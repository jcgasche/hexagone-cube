Hexagone³ was written by Jean-Charles Gasche, independently and unpaid. He has no link with SNCF, SNCF Réseau, Île-de-France Mobilités, the State, the Region or the City of Paris, and nobody commissioned or funded the study. Its text, figures, data and model are free to reuse with credit.

The author

Jean-Charles Gasche wrote the study in his own time, to show that through stations under Paris deserve a professional study. The layout and every choice in it are his. The model, the drawings and the text were built with the help of an AI assistant (Claude, by Anthropic); every figure comes from the open model, which anyone can rerun.

Contact and corrections

To point out an error, suggest a better source or discuss the plan, open an issue on the study's GitHub page. What a correction changes is carried into the model and into both languages; see assumptions and limits for the points that most need checking.

Reusing the study

The text, figures, data and model of Hexagone³ are under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence (CC BY 4.0): anyone may copy, share and adapt them, for any purpose, provided they credit the author, link to the study and say what they changed.

To cite it: Jean-Charles Gasche, Hexagone³: through stations under Paris, version 4.1, October 2026, https://jcgasche.github.io/hexagone-cube/.

The source

The site, the model and the data are published on GitHub, with every version dated. The model is a set of Python scripts with no packages to install; the page explains how to rerun it and how every number in the text is filled in from its results (see how the numbers are made).
