The idea

From four dead ends to one triangle

Today: every train stops at the end of the line

Four high-speed terminals, one for each direction. Trains come in, wait, and go back out the way they came. To go on, you take the metro across Paris to another station.

  • 118 platform tracks at the four terminals, and still full in the busy hours
  • Track fields that cut the north, the east and the south of Paris

The TGV triangle

Gare du Nord and Gare de l'Est merge into one deep station, Paris Nord-Est. With Gare de Lyon and Montparnasse, three through stations are joined by one twin-track tunnel on each side of the triangle.

  • {{ m.run_min | 0 }} minutes from any station to any other
  • Every station has direct trains to every direction: north, east, south-east and Atlantic
  • No train reverses: each one stops twice and goes on

Regional and suburban trains too

Regional express trains run in the TGV tunnels, in the gaps between the TGVs, and on to the far side of France's regions. Two suburban tunnels join the suburbs across Paris.

  • N lines P and N merged, from the north and the east through Nord-Est to Montparnasse
  • H lines H, K and L, through Nord-Est to a deep station under Saint-Lazare
  • The surface terminals close; their tracks become parks

How

The terminals go underground, as in Zürich and London

The surface stations disappear: their platforms move to deep through stations right under the old halls, and the tunnels come up at the ring road, on today's tracks.

Reaching the station

Two stations for every train, so one is nearer

Each train stops at two of the three stations. If you live in the south of Paris, your train to Lille now stops at Montparnasse or Gare de Lyon: no more trek to Gare du Nord. From the average address in Paris, {{ pts.avg.today | 1 }} minutes become {{ pts.avg.station | 1 }}, and no address is further than today.
