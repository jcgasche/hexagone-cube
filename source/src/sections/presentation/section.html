The rail connector of all France, under Paris

Hexagone3

A connector for every region of France. Three through stations under Paris, joined by three tunnels, link every line to every other, with the regular, reliable timetable that made the Swiss railways a model. And the capital gets a rare chance: four great parks, freed land and quicker journeys.

The idea

From four dead ends to one triangle

Today: every train stops at the end of the line

Four high-speed terminals, one for each direction. Trains come in, wait, and go back out the way they came. To go on, you take the metro across Paris to another station.

  • 118 platform tracks at the four terminals, and still full in the busy hours
  • Track fields that cut the north, the east and the south of Paris

The TGV triangle

Gare du Nord and Gare de l'Est merge into one deep station, Paris Nord-Est. With Gare de Lyon and Montparnasse, three through stations are joined by one twin-track tunnel on each side of the triangle.

  • {{ m.run_min | 0 }} minutes from any station to any other
  • Every station has direct trains to every direction: north, east, south-east and Atlantic
  • No train reverses: each one stops twice and goes on

With TER and Transilien

Regional express trains (TER) run in the TGV tunnels, in the gaps between the TGVs, and on through Paris to the regions beyond. The suburban Transilien lines become new RER lines, crossing Paris instead of ending there.

  • G RER G: today's lines P and N merge into one line, from the north-east and the east through Nord-Est and Châtelet-Les Halles to Montparnasse
  • F RER F: today's lines L, H and K merge into one line, through Nord-Est and a deep Saint-Lazare to La Défense
  • The surface tracks close, apart from a few for line R at Gare de Lyon and line J at Saint-Lazare, and become parks

For all of France

Across France with no change in Paris

Each change of train makes about {{ m.params.change_loss | pct }} of travellers give up. Today, crossing France by Paris means two changes: off the train, across the city by metro, onto another train, about {{ d.french.params.x_today | 0 }} minutes in Paris. Only a few pairs of cities have direct trains, which go round Paris a few times a day. With the triangle every train runs through Paris: with the same number of trains as today, the direct links between the regions multiply, and every journey is shorter.

The suburbs joined across Paris

The jobs are on the far side of Paris. Two new RER lines take commuters there without a change.

The regional lines (TER) on either side are joined too, so the neighbouring regions work as one whole. People in the outer areas gain what Parisians gain: more direct links to the rest of France and to the other outer areas, so more choice of where to work and where to live.

Paris at the centre, for the whole country

Every main line of France leads to Paris, and today Paris is where they stop: a wall between the regions. Hexagone³ accepts that Paris is at the centre of the network and makes use of it. Through stations turn the dead ends into a crossroads, so the regions are joined to each other without depending on a change in the capital, and Paris gains parks, space and quicker trips at the same time.
