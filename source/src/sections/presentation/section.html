The idea

From four dead ends to one triangle

Today: every train stops at the end of the line

Four high-speed terminals, one for each direction. Trains come in, wait, and go back out the way they came. To go on, you take the metro across Paris to another station.

  • 118 platform tracks at the four terminals, and still full in the busy hours
  • Track fields that cut the north, the east and the south of Paris

The TGV triangle

Gare du Nord and Gare de l'Est merge into one deep station, Paris Nord-Est. With Gare de Lyon and Montparnasse, three through stations are joined by one twin-track tunnel on each side of the triangle.

  • {{ m.run_min | 0 }} minutes from any station to any other
  • Every station has direct trains to every direction: north, east, south-east and Atlantic
  • No train reverses: each one stops twice and goes on

With TER and Transilien

Regional express trains (TER) run in the TGV tunnels, in the gaps between the TGVs, and on to the other side of the country. The suburban Transilien lines become new RER lines, crossing Paris instead of ending there.

  • G RER G: today's lines P and N merge into one line, from the north and the east through Nord-Est to Montparnasse
  • F RER F: today's lines L, H and K merge into one line, through Nord-Est and a deep Saint-Lazare to La Défense
  • The surface terminals close; their tracks become parks

For all of France

Across France with no change in Paris

Today, crossing France by Paris means getting off, taking the metro to another station and waiting for the next train. With the triangle, the train runs through, and every pair of cities gets a train every hour instead of a few a day.

Today's time counts the change of station in Paris as {{ d.french.params.x_today | 0 }} minutes from train to train, as the model does. A few of these pairs also have direct trains that go round Paris, a handful a day; with the project every pair has {{ d.french.params.proj_per_day | 0 }} through trains a day, one an hour.

Reaching the station

Two stations for every train, so one is nearer

Each train stops at two of the three stations. If you live in the south of Paris, your train to Lille now stops at Montparnasse or Gare de Lyon: no more trek to Gare du Nord. Every address in Paris is as close to its train as today or closer, up to {{ pts.max_gain.station | 1 }} minutes closer; on average {{ pts.avg.today | 1 }} minutes become {{ pts.avg.station | 1 }}.
