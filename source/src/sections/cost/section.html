No design exists yet, so the cost is built from quantities (kilometres of tunnel, metres of platform) and from what stations and tunnels have cost once built. The project comes to about €{{ m.cost.total | 0 }} billion, between €{{ m.cost_low | 0 }} billion and €{{ m.cost_high | 0 }} billion with low and high prices. The four deep stations, with the RER G platforms at Châtelet-Les Halles, are the largest item; the tunnels come next: one for the TGVs, shared with the regional express trains, and one for each RER line. The regional express trains have no tunnel of their own: running in the TGV tunnels costs only the links that lead them in.

Regional express trains in the TGV tunnels

The regional express trains (TER) share the TGV tunnels instead of having a tunnel of their own. Paths in the tunnels are two minutes apart, {{ m.ter_paths.paths_per_hour | 0 }} an hour each way; the TGVs keep their places in the 12-minute beat and the regional express trains take the paths in between (see the service). This needs signalling for trains about 1.5 minutes apart assumption. What must still be built is the way in: the regional express tracks have to reach the TGV tracks at both ends of the two stations where they have platforms of their own, Nord-Est and Montparnasse, and the existing regional lines have to reach the tunnels at their portals. The item is €{{ m.cost.items['TER links into the TGV tunnels'] | 2 }} billion before the add-ons, €{{ m.cost.items['TER links into the TGV tunnels'] * m.gross_up | 2 }} billion all-in (derived) assumption:

  • six tie-ins in the station throats, where the regional express tracks join the TGV tracks (four at Nord-Est, two at Montparnasse), €0.05 billion each;
  • two dive-unders in the throats of Nord-Est, where the eastern and Gare de Lyon regional trains pass under the TGV lines, €0.10 billion each;
  • links at three TGV portals (north, east, Atlantic), where the regional lines join the tunnel ramps, €0.05 billion each.

No project publishes a price for such links; the figures are assumptions, set below the price of a portal or a junction because each is a smaller work. The tunnels, junctions and portals are those of the TGV, so the regional express trains add no kilometre of tunnel.

Open item: line R. Line R stays on the surface at Gare de Lyon, on a small terminal. Running it through to an eastern line would need two more tracks at Gare de Lyon and two at Nord-Est, about €{{ m.ter_paths.r_option.cost | 1 }} billion all-in (derived), for {{ m.ter_paths.r_option.trains | 0 }} trains an hour with no proven partner; it is left as a later option and not costed in the total.

Why {{ m.rates.risk | pct }}

The usual allowance for a study estimate is about 30%, and that is what this estimate carried at first. It is now {{ m.rates.risk | pct }}, for three reasons.

  • The unit prices are final costs or signed contracts, not early estimates. The first RER E figure is the final cost of the line, which includes its 44% overrun.[[cgpc]] The RER E stations of 2024 are the contracts of 2016 to 2018.[[eolekit, d92cnit]] Zürich is a final cost;[[zh]] London is the cost of the station contracts as forecast near the end of the works.[[nao19]] The Grand Paris figures are contracts and the files revised in 2017.[[sgpi, d92l15, senat44]] A price that already holds an overrun needs a smaller margin than a price taken from a study.
  • The earlier figure counted some things twice. The station price was first grossed up by 1.27, then by 1.30, for design, land and risk, before 30% was added again on everything. For the stations that counted design and land partly twice. The price is now the all-in figure from Paris, and the add-ons are applied once.
  • The back-test says where the prices sit. Before risk, they come out {{ m.backtest[0].model_no_risk / m.backtest[0].actual - 1 | pct }} above the first RER E section and {{ m.backtest[5].model_no_risk / m.backtest[5].actual - 1 | pct }} above Zürich, but {{ 1 - m.backtest[1].model_no_risk / m.backtest[1].actual | pct }} to {{ 1 - m.backtest[1].model_no_risk / m.backtest[1].actual_high | pct }} below the RER E extension and far below Munich (see does the method hold).

What the {{ m.rates.risk | pct }} still covers: stations deeper than any built in Paris, under terminals that stay open throughout; and the items for which no project publishes a price (junction caverns, portals, the conversion of the halls), {{ (m.cost.items['Underground junctions'] + m.cost.items['Portals and links to existing lines'] + m.cost.items['Historic halls']) / m.cost.hard | pct }} of the works. Two study points remain: a check, item by item, of which published prices are all-in (I9), and a risk allowance set item by item instead of one rate on everything (I10).

Does the method hold?

The same prices were applied to seven projects that have been built or priced, and compared with their published cost.

  • Paris, first RER E section, built. The prices overshoot: €{{ m.backtest[0].model_no_risk | 1 }} billion before risk, €{{ m.backtest[0].model_with_risk | 1 }} billion with it, against a final cost of €{{ m.backtest[0].actual | 1 }} billion that already includes a 44% overrun. The index used to bring 2003 money to 2026 is a floor, so the gap is smaller than it looks.
  • Paris, RER E extension, built. The prices undershoot: €{{ m.backtest[1].model_no_risk | 1 }} billion before risk against €{{ m.backtest[1].actual | 1 }} to €{{ m.backtest[1].actual_high | 1 }} billion, depending on the price base of the published figure. With {{ m.rates.risk | pct }} risk, €{{ m.backtest[1].model_with_risk | 1 }} billion, still {{ 1 - m.backtest[1].model_with_risk / m.backtest[1].actual | pct }} below the low reading. This is the project most like the one proposed, and the one the prices miss on the low side.
  • Lyon and Marseille, study estimates. Before risk the prices straddle what SNCF Réseau estimates for a deep four-track station with its tunnels: €{{ m.backtest[2].model_no_risk | 1 }} billion against €{{ m.backtest[2].actual | 1 }} billion for Lyon, €{{ m.backtest[3].model_no_risk | 1 }} billion against €{{ m.backtest[3].actual | 1 }} billion for Marseille, a figure that covers a whole phase of works. With risk, both are above the estimates, as a built cost tends to be: those estimates carry a contingency of their own, and in Paris such estimates have run 45% over.
  • Stuttgart, building. With {{ m.rates.risk | pct }} risk the prices land on the current forecast (€{{ m.backtest[4].model_with_risk | 1 }} billion against €{{ m.backtest[4].actual | 1 }} billion), which is in money of each year and would be higher in 2026 euros.
  • Zürich, built. The prices overshoot a final cost: €{{ m.backtest[5].model_no_risk | 1 }} billion before risk against €{{ m.backtest[5].actual | 1 }} billion. Zürich built a four-track station only 16 m down, under a working terminal, for half the Paris price per metre.
  • Munich, building. The prices give less than half the forecast (€{{ m.backtest[6].model_with_risk | 1 }} billion with risk against €{{ m.backtest[6].actual | 1 }} billion, which includes future inflation). Three stations 40 m under the centre are what a Paris project would look like if everything went wrong.
The reading

The prices are those of built projects, and the {{ m.rates.risk | pct }} is what separates the record's middle from its cheapest cases. It is not a cushion for an early estimate, because the prices already hold the overruns of the projects they come from. The spread between Zürich and Munich is the real uncertainty, and the RER E extension, the closest precedent, sits on the high side of the prices.

The cost per year

To compare a cost paid once with money and benefits that come every year, the construction cost is turned into a yearly repayment, like a loan repaid over 60 years at 3% above inflation. Money is spent for years before the first train runs: over {{ m.params.build_years | 0 }} years of even spending assumption, interest during the works adds {{ m.idc - 1 | pct }}. Each €1 billion of construction then costs €{{ m.AF * m.idc * 1000 | 0 }} million a year.
