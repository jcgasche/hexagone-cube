What the timetable rests on

  • Spacing in the tunnels. {{ d.timetable.table.params.headway | 0 }} minutes between two trains on one track is about what the south-east line needs to carry the {{ m.hour_full.Southeast | 0 }} trains an hour planned for it,[[lgvse]] and more than the 2.5 minutes of the Elizabeth line's central tunnel at 24 trains an hour.[[el_may23]] The beat of {{ d.timetable.table.grid.params.step | 0 }} minutes keeps the TGVs further apart than that; the TER between them bring the spacing down to {{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.full.measures.min_gap | g }} minutes ({{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.max.measures.min_gap | g }} at {{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.max.measures.close | 0 }} places in the hour with Nord-Est filled too), which is why the tunnels need signalling for about {{ m.ter_paths.signal_min[0] | 1 }} (see below).
  • Regional express trains (TER) run in the TGV tunnels, on an hour of their own fitted between the TGVs by the model (model/ter_timetable.py, a constraint solver; model/table_timetable.py checks its result again against the TGV hour). They take the times between the TGV places, in half-minutes: on every track a TER shares, the train ahead and the train behind are at least {{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.full.measures.min_gap | g }} minutes away with the southern lines full ({{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.today.measures.min_gap | g }} about today; with Nord-Est filled too, {{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.max.measures.min_gap | g }} at {{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.max.measures.close | 0 }} places in the hour), which needs signalling for trains about {{ m.ter_paths.signal_min[0] | 1 }} minutes apart assumption. A TER stops {{ m.reg_platforms.ter.stop | 0 }} minutes and its platform track then stays empty {{ m.reg_platforms.ter.gap | g }}; where it has to wait for its path it stands longer: {{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.full.measures.longer_stops | 0 }} of the hour's {{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.full.measures.stops | 0 }} TER stops, up to {{ d.timetable.table.grid.regional.full.measures.max_stop | g }} minutes. The TER stop at platforms of their own at Nord-Est and Montparnasse and on the fourth high-speed track each way at Gare de Lyon. RER G (today's lines P and N) has its own tunnel from Nord-Est to Montparnasse, and RER F (today's lines H, K and L) its own from Nord-Est to Saint-Lazare. These trains, described in how many platforms, take no place from the high-speed trains. How many TER each line would run through Paris is study point I3 in what to verify. Suburban line R stays on the surface at Gare de Lyon.
  • Trains on time. A late train delays the train behind it on a full line, and the change between the north and the east. Regular timetables rest on punctual trains: in Switzerland more than nine in ten arrive within three minutes;[[ch_sbbfacts]] see the Swiss timetable applied to Paris.
  • Destinations. The timetable fixes paths, not destinations: whether a path runs to Bordeaux or to Nantes, and how often each city is served, is a choice of service. The waits above are for the next train to a line, or between two lines.
  • Junctions. Trains crossing each other at the junctions north and south of Nord-Est and at the ends of the link are taken to do so on separate levels; the cost counts {{ m.cost.junctions | 0 }} junctions (see the prices used).
