This study is an independent pre-feasibility case, written by one person outside any rail company or public agency. It shows that the idea deserves a professional study; it does not show that it can be built as drawn. This chapter sets out what rests on data, what is assumed, and what only a professional study can settle.

What this study is, and what it is not

What it is

  • A complete first estimate: the layout, an hour of trains, the cost from built projects, the traffic and an appraisal under the French rules.
  • Open: every figure is computed by a small model published with the site, which anyone can rerun or change (see the source).
  • Written to be checked: each chapter states its sources, and each figure that has none is marked as an assumption.

What it is not

  • An engineering design: no alignment has been surveyed, no ground investigated, no station drawn to the standards of SNCF Réseau.
  • A tested timetable: the hour of trains is drawn by hand and by a small solver, not simulated with the signalling.
  • An official document: no agency commissioned, reviewed or endorsed it, whether SNCF, SNCF Réseau, Île-de-France Mobilités, the State, the Region or the City of Paris.

How far the result can move

In the central case, the benefits counted return {{ d.french.study.central.cover | pct }} of the yearly cost, and the French reading gives +€{{ d.french.cases.central.npv | 1 }}bn net in today's money. The uncertain items together take that from +€{{ d.french.cases.low.npv | 1 }}bn to +€{{ d.french.cases.high.npv | 1 }}bn. The result is therefore a case for a professional study, not yet a case for building.

What a professional study would add

  • Ground surveys, and alignments for the tunnels, the junctions and the portals.
  • The timetable simulated with the signalling, under the operating rules of SNCF Réseau.
  • A traffic model built on ticket data and the trip surveys of SNCF and Île-de-France Mobilités.
  • Construction methods and stages under stations that stay open, with the disruption they cause.
  • Heritage, environment and noise during the works.
  • Who would own, build, run and pay for each part.
