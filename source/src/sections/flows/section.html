This chapter maps how many people ride each railway line of the Paris region, section by section: RER, Transilien, TER and TGV, today and with the project. The widths come from counts: the boardings counted at the stations of each line, set on the timetable of one Tuesday. The busiest pair of tracks is the tunnel that RER B and D share between Châtelet and Gare du Nord, with about {{ d.flows.k.bd | 0 }} passengers a day. With the project, the RER G tunnel beside it would carry about {{ d.flows.k.g | 0 }}.

The map

Each line is as wide as the passengers it carries in a day, both ways together. Where branches meet, their widths add up; where a line parts, they divide. Lines that run side by side are drawn side by side, so the width of a bundle is the traffic of the corridor.

The second view adds up the lines that use the same pair of tracks, and colours each section by how full it is. Two measures are offered. The first sets the passengers of the busiest hour against the places on the trains of that hour. The second sets the trains of that hour against the trains the section can take; it is published for very few sections, and the others stay grey.

Line by line

The counts cover {{ d.flows.key.stations | 0 }} stations and {{ d.flows.k.counted | 0 }} boardings a working day. For most lines the count gives the whole line. Three lines need a published total as well: RER A and RER B, because the stations of the RATP in Paris are not counted line by line, and RER E, because its count is older than its extension to Nanterre.

The last column needs the size of the trains, taken from what their makers and operators publish for a full-length train.[[alstom_mi09, alstom_mi20, sncf_d_ng, sncf_rerng, sncf_francilien]] No figure was found for the trains of RER C and of lines N, R, U and V: those lines stay grey in the view by track.

How the map is made, and what is missing

No one publishes how many passengers ride each section of a line. The map is built from three things that are published.

  • Boardings at each station. The SNCF counts the passengers who board its Transilien and RER trains, station by station and line by line.[[sncf_count]] For the RATP stations of RER A and B, the entries at the gates stand in, at the stations that one line serves.[[idfm_valid]]
  • Where they go. The census gives the journeys to work between every pair of communes.[[mobpro]] Each of the {{ d.flows.key.commuters / 1000000 | 1 }} million commuters by public transport is put on the quickest route through the timetable, metro included. That gives, for each line, the share of riders between each pair of stations.
  • The fit. Those shares are scaled, station by station, until each station has the boardings counted there. The passengers of a section are those of all the pairs of stations that ride over it. Stations without a count are set so that the line carries its published total.
  • Trains. Trains a day and in the busiest hour are counted on the timetable of Tuesday 3 November 2026, section by section.[[idfm_counts]]

How full. The passengers of the busiest hour, in the busier direction, are the day's passengers times the share of a day's entries made in the busiest morning hour, 11% to 14% on most lines,[[idfm_profiles]] times the share who ride that way in the morning. They are set against the trains of that hour, each taken as a full-length train assumption. Short trains, and passengers who all choose the same cars, make real trains fuller than this.

With the project. Today's lines keep today's passengers: the growth of the chapter Traffic is not added, so that the two maps show the same travellers. The TGV and TER tunnels carry what the model of this study gives them: the TER passengers who ride through, and the TGV passengers who board at another station or cross Paris.

The tunnels of RER F and RER G. Their passengers come from the calculation of travel times in the region. Every person who goes to work by public transport takes the quickest route on the network of the plan, with lines 15 to 18 in service, and the calculation counts who rides through each tunnel: {{ d.flows.k.engine.commuters.f | 0 }} people each morning for RER F, {{ d.flows.k.engine.commuters.g | 0 }} for RER G north of Châtelet. One of these people stands for {{ d.flows.k.engine.factor | 2 }} passengers a day assumption. That is the ratio found today where lines P, N, H and K enter their Paris terminals: {{ d.flows.k.engine.counted | 0 }} passengers counted a day, for {{ d.flows.k.engine.modelled | 0 }} people in the same calculation on today's network. The figure is on the low side: in the centre of Paris, where more trips are made for other reasons than work, the ratio is higher.

What is missing, and how it shows.

  • TER and Intercités by line. Only totals by terminal are published, and none for Saint-Lazare, Austerlitz and Bercy. Those lines are dashed grey. Elsewhere the total is shared out by the trains of the regional timetable, which lists only part of the TER trains.
  • TGV by branch. The regulator publishes passengers by group of lines, not by line or by pair of cities. Each line keeps one width from Paris to the edge of the map; the lines round Paris are dashed grey.
  • Tracks. The open data do not say which trains share which pair of tracks. In Paris each RER tunnel is taken as one pair. Elsewhere each RER line is taken alone, and the Transilien and TER trains of a section together.
  • Capacity. The trains a section can take are published for the shared tunnel of RER B and D, the tunnel of RER E and the south-east TGV line. Every other section is grey in that view.
  • Other trips than commuting. Journeys to work decide where the counted passengers are sent. Students, shoppers, tourists and airport passengers are counted at their station, then sent like commuters.
  • Years. The counts date from 2022 to 2025 according to the line, and the timetable from 2026.
