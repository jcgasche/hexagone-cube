The case rests on a handful of things nobody has yet measured or drawn. Cost and the time needed to board in a deep station move the economic result most; the traffic that crosses Paris, more than running costs, decides how far the project pays its way once open. Most of these can be settled by studies that cost little next to the project, and twenty-seven study points are listed at the end. Running regional express trains in the TGV tunnels rests on one condition: signalling that lets trains follow each other about {{ m.ter_paths.signal_min[0] | 1 }} minutes apart. Line R at Gare de Lyon stays on the surface; running it through is left as a later option.

How sure is the result?

Each line changes one assumption of the station model and shows the share of the yearly cost that the benefits counted then cover: the complete count of the economic return, held flat at today's traffic. The central case is {{ d.french.study.central.cover | pct }}. The official reading, with growth and the French rules, has its own tests in what could change the answer.

The one test that changes only the project's income, 10% more TGV traffic once the platforms no longer cap the lines, is left out of the chart and shown in the financing chapter; the room for more trains is valued in the official reading only, as the journeys the terminals would turn away (see what could change the answer).

  • Cost matters most. The same benefits cover {{ d.french.study.central.tests['High unit prices and 30% risk'].cover | pct }} to {{ d.french.study.central.tests['Low unit prices and 10% risk'].cover | pct }} of the cost depending on prices and risk.
  • Boarding time comes next. Two minutes more for every passenger removes {{ 1 - d.french.study.central.tests['Every passenger takes 2 minutes longer to board than today'].benefits / d.french.study.central.benefit_total | pct }} of the benefits: an extra {{ 0 - m.access.central.eq.all / 2 | 1 }} minutes on the clock is enough to cancel the whole gain in access. This is why the design of the Nord-Est station is the first thing to study (see inside the station).
  • How access is counted matters less. TGV passengers are counted at the station entrance. If the train’s ride between its Paris stops were counted against them, even with each passenger picking the train that calls at their best station, benefits would cover {{ d.french.study.central.tests['TGV passengers’ ride across Paris counted against them'].cover | pct }} of the cost assumption, study point I16.
  • Growth over time counts. Benefits are held flat in this chart. The official reading lets long-distance traffic grow {{ d.french.params.ld_growth | pct1 }} a year and the value of time {{ d.french.params.vot_growth | pct1 }} to {{ d.french.params.last | str }}: +€{{ d.french.cases.central.npv | 1 }}bn net in today's money, while the official stress test with no growth at a 4% rate gives a negative value of €{{ -d.french.cases.no_growth_4.npv | 1 }}bn (see what could change the answer); growth needs the capacity the project is meant to unlock.
  • The employment corridors are counted on the census. Commuters who gain a through suburban line across Paris, mainly from the northern suburbs to La Défense, Courbevoie and Levallois and from Meaux to Versailles and Saint-Quentin, are worth €{{ d.french.study.central.benefits[d.french.names.key.job_users] | m }}m a year at today's values, €{{ d.french.timeline.jobs.users[12] | m }}m in the opening year, rising as homes and jobs move towards the access; the region's travel model would confirm it (study point I14).

Stages and the decision

The project can be built in two stages: first the TGV part of Paris Nord-Est and Gare de Lyon, with its eight tracks, the north, east and south-east TGV tunnels and the tunnel between the two stations, together with RER F from the north through Nord-Est to Saint-Lazare and most of the links that bring regional express trains into the TGV tunnels, {{ m.stages.first.km | 1 }} km of twin-track tunnel for €{{ m.stages.first.total | 1 }} billion all-in; then Montparnasse, with the TGV tunnels to Nord-Est and the Atlantic, the direct link, RER G with its stop at Châtelet-Les Halles and the regional express platforms at Nord-Est, {{ m.stages.second.km | 1 }} km for €{{ m.stages.second.total | 1 }} billion; see works and stages.

The first stage serves the trains of three of the four high-speed lines and the northern suburban lines. Its benefits on its own have not been estimated, so this study cannot say whether stopping after it would be a better or a worse project, and the staging itself is study point I8: the order, what each stage leaves usable, and whether the link can wait.

What to decide now

Not whether to build. The decision today is whether to spend on eight quick tests that can confirm or stop the case:

  1. Ask for ticket data on passengers who change station in Paris, by origin and destination (study point I12).
  2. Draw one standard hour of trains with the track owner (SNCF Réseau): every TGV stopping at two of the three stations, six tracks a station, no train reversing (I2, I3).
  3. Commission a concept design of the Nord-Est station with its {{ m.cost.tracks['Paris Nord-Est'] | 0 }} tracks ({{ m.cost.detail['Paris Nord-Est'].hs | 0 }} TGV, {{ m.cost.detail['Paris Nord-Est'].ter | 0 }} regional express, {{ m.cost.detail['Paris Nord-Est'].sub | 0 }} suburban): position, depth, time from hall to train, and a first engineer's price (I2, I9).
  4. Ask what deep stations and tunnels cost to run, from the records of RER E and of the stations opened in 2024, net of what today's terminals cost (I11). How large the surplus of operations is turns on it.
  5. Ask when each terminal and each line is full, with and without the project (I1). The official reading values the room for more trains at €{{ d.french.params.cap_value | 0 }} a journey the terminals would turn away, €{{ d.french.cases.central.pv[d.french.names.key.capacity] | 1 }}bn in today's money, an assumption; the view of the track owner would replace it.
  6. Run the region's travel model on the two through suburban lines, RER F and RER G: suburb to suburb trips, the jobs within reach along each corridor, relief of RER E, frequency (I14, I26).
  7. Measure the parks: a survey of the track fields, and a decision of the City on what is built and how many visits the parks can expect (I19); and decide whether line R should later run through (I21).
  8. Ask the track owner whether the TGV tunnels can be signalled for trains about {{ m.ter_paths.signal_min[0] | 1 }} minutes apart, with ETCS level 2, short blocks and automatic driving (I27). The regional express trains in the TGV tunnels depend on it.

With those in hand, settle the level and the legal form of the passenger charges (D7, I15), fix the stages (I8), and take the case to a full appraisal.

The case in three sentences
  • Operations pay for themselves and leave a surplus. With €{{ fp.charge_end | 2 }} on TGV passengers who use a station their line did not serve, €{{ fp.charge_through | 2 }} on those who cross Paris, the same on regional express passengers who use the second station, the track charges on new journeys and half of the operators' saving on trainsets, income covers {{ f.central.ops_cover | pct }} of running costs ({{ f.high_running.ops_cover | pct }} to {{ f.low_running.ops_cover | pct }} across the running-cost range, {{ f.low_traffic.ops_cover | pct }} in the low traffic case).
  • Construction needs public money for most of its cost. The benefits counted are worth {{ d.french.study.central.cover | pct }} of the yearly cost, and +€{{ d.french.cases.central.npv | 1 }}bn net in today's money under the French rules, but they pass through no budget; a business tax package, a European grant and a grant of €{{ f.central.grant_bn | 1 }} billion add up to {{ f.central.public_share | pct }} of the €{{ m.cost.total | 1 }} billion.
  • The judgement rests on the least firm items. Most of the benefits are measured on the project's own traffic; the room for more trains in the busy hours, the regular timetable and the later arrival at through platforms are assumptions with ranges. Without any one of them the net value stays positive (+€{{ d.french.cases.no_capacity.npv | 1 }}bn, +€{{ d.french.cases.no_timetable.npv | 1 }}bn and +€{{ d.french.cases.no_margin.npv | 1 }}bn), and the uncertain items together take it from +€{{ d.french.cases.low.npv | 1 }}bn to +€{{ d.french.cases.high.npv | 1 }}bn.
