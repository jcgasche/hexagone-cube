This annex takes the economic return of the plan apart. It gives the return of the TGV and TER triangle, of RER F and of RER G, each against its own cost, and shows what the order of the openings changes. The rules are those of the economic return, and the parts add up to the plan item by item. Nothing here changes the plan or its figures.

How the plan is shared out. Each cost goes to the part it builds: the tunnels by their length, the platform tracks, the portals and the junctions by the trains that use them. Each benefit goes to the travellers it belongs to. The TGV and regional express travellers go to the triangle, the riders of lines H, K and L to RER F, the riders of lines P and N to RER G. The freed surface needs every part at once: the tracks of a terminal are cleared only when all its trains run underground. It is shown on a line of its own.

The triangle carries the plan. The three TGV tunnels and their stations, with the regional express trains that run in them, cost €{{ d.parts.parts.T.capex | 1 }}bn and leave a net gain of €{{ d.parts.parts.T.npv | 1 }}bn. That is more than the net gain of the whole plan, €{{ d.parts.combos.plan.npv | 1 }}bn.

RER F pays for itself. Its largest gain is that of the commuters who get a through line across Paris, €{{ d.parts.k.F.job | 1 }}bn in today's money. Then come the crowded metro and RER legs its riders no longer take (€{{ d.parts.k.F.crowd | 1 }}bn) and the Paris side of their trips (€{{ d.parts.k.F.reg | 1 }}bn). With the freed surface counted on its side, it returns {{ d.parts.combos['F+S'].ratio | 2 }}.

RER G brings back less than half of its cost. It carries half as many trains as RER F in a tunnel almost twice as long. Its gains are the relief of line 4 and of the metro and RER in Paris (€{{ d.parts.k.G.g_crowd | 1 }}bn), the stop at Châtelet-Les Halles (€{{ d.parts.k.G.g_stop | 1 }}bn), the Paris side of its riders' trips (€{{ d.parts.k.G.reg | 1 }}bn) and the commuters who get a through line (€{{ d.parts.k.G.job | 1 }}bn). RER G is also what clears the surface of Gare de l'Est and of Montparnasse; with the freed surface on its side it returns {{ d.parts.combos['G+S'].ratio | 2 }}.

What the suburban tunnels lack. RER F, RER G and the freed surface together return {{ d.parts.combos['F+G+S'].ratio | 2 }}. They would pay for themselves with €{{ d.parts.need.to_pay_year * 1000 | 0 }}m more of benefits a year, and match the return of the whole plan with €{{ d.parts.need.to_match_plan_year * 1000 | 0 }}m more.

Between RER F and RER G, the gap rests on one model. Measured station to station on the timetable, the two tunnels save the same time: {{ d.times.v.plan_rer_f.hours_day | 0 }} hours a working day for RER F alone and {{ d.times.v.plan_rer_g.hours_day | 0 }} for RER G alone (see Travel times in the region). The economic return does not use those hours. It uses the count of jobs within reach, which adds the new trips and gives more weight to a change of train avoided, and which finds most of the gain between the north and the west of the region. This is the least firm part of the split.

With the whole triangle first, the net gain is €{{ d.parts.stages.triangle.npv - d.parts.stages.counted.npv | 1 }}bn higher. The three TGV tunnels and the four stations open together in {{ d.parts.years.first | str }}, and every TGV and regional express train runs through from that day. RER F and RER G follow in {{ d.parts.years.counted | str }}. The part that returns the most earns three years sooner, and it no longer waits for the others.

It fits the same yearly budget. The triangle costs €{{ d.parts.spend.triangle_by_2036 | 1 }}bn. At the plan's rate of spending, €{{ d.parts.spend.spent_by_2036_at_the_plan_rate | 1 }}bn is spent in the first nine years. Opening RER F with the triangle would add €{{ d.parts.stages.triangle_f.npv - d.parts.stages.triangle.npv | 1 }}bn of net gain, and need €{{ d.parts.parts.T.capex + d.parts.parts.F.capex | 1 }}bn in those nine years.

What it asks of the works. The four stations are in works at the same time, where the Works chapter takes Montparnasse after the others. The Atlantic trains keep their surface platforms until the opening, as the others do. Whether the sites, the machines and the closures allow it is a question for the builders; it is not studied here.

The date of the suburban tunnels weighs little. With the triangle open in {{ d.parts.years.first | str }}, opening RER F and RER G five or ten years after {{ d.parts.years.counted | str }} leaves €{{ d.parts.stages.later5.npv | 1 }}bn and €{{ d.parts.stages.later10.npv | 1 }}bn. The parks of Gare du Nord, Gare de l'Est, Saint-Lazare and Montparnasse wait for them.

RER B and RER D: the room given back is already counted, and trains on time add about €{{ d.parts.bd.plan.year * 1000 | 1 }}m a year

The plan takes {{ d.parts.relief.plan.both | pct }} of the commuters out of the tunnel of RER B and D. One morning, {{ d.parts.relief.plan.b | pct }} of those of RER B and {{ d.parts.relief.plan.d | pct }} of those of RER D find a quicker way by RER G or RER F (see Passengers by line).

Their comfort is in the economic return. The riders who leave a crowded leg are counted, and the riders left on board gain {{ d.french.params.external | pct }} as much again. Together the two lines of the economic return on crowding are worth €{{ (d.parts.counted_crowding.g_crowd_year + d.parts.counted_crowding.crowd_year) * 1000 | 0 }}m a year at the opening, of which €{{ d.parts.counted_crowding.g_crowd_year * 1000 | 0 }}m for the relief by RER G.

What is not counted is small: trains closer to time. With fewer people at the doors, the stops in the shared tunnel are shorter and the trains hold each other up less. The simulation of the tunnel (see the annex on RER D) gives the delay a train picks up there: from {{ d.parts.bd.base.b_s | 0 }} to {{ d.parts.bd.plan.b_s | 0 }} seconds on RER B and from {{ d.parts.bd.base.d_s | 0 }} to {{ d.parts.bd.plan.d_s | 0 }} on RER D, if the time beyond {{ d.parts.bd.doors_s | 0 }} seconds of each stop falls with the number of riders assumption. That is {{ d.parts.bd.plan.minutes_day | 0 }} fewer minutes of delay a day for the riders who stay, about €{{ d.parts.bd.plan.year * 1000 | 1 }}m a year. In the variant with line L and RER C, which takes {{ d.parts.relief.variant.both | pct }} of the commuters out, it is about €{{ d.parts.bd.variant.year * 1000 | 0 }}m a year.

More trains in the suburban tunnels cost more than they bring, at today's demand. RER G runs {{ d.parts.room.G['from'] | 0 }} trains an hour on platforms that take {{ d.parts.room.G.platform_limit | 0 }}, and RER F {{ d.parts.room.F['from'] | 0 }}. Four more trains an hour on RER G would save its riders €{{ d.parts.room.G.gain * 1000 | 0 }}m a year in waits and room, and cost €{{ d.parts.room.G.running * 1000 | 0 }}m a year to run assumption. On RER F it is €{{ d.parts.room.F.gain * 1000 | 0 }}m against €{{ d.parts.room.F.running * 1000 | 0 }}m. The room is worth something the day the lines fill up; it is not a gain that can be counted now.

What these figures rest on

  • The same model as the economic return, cut differently. The parts add up to the plan for every item: €{{ d.parts.combos.plan.npv | 1 }}bn of net gain and {{ d.parts.combos.plan.ratio | 2 }} for each euro. Every caution of the economic return applies to each part.
  • A part alone is not a project. The shares say where the return comes from. They do not say what the triangle would cost or bring if it were designed without the suburban tunnels: Nord-Est would be dug differently, and the surface of the terminals would stay in use.
  • The first stage's share of the gains is an assumption. It is set item by item from the lines and the trips across Paris that the first stage serves. No timetable of the first stage alone has been drawn.
  • The suburban gain is shared between RER F and RER G by one figure. The count of jobs within reach gives RER G {{ d.parts.k.G.job / (d.parts.k.G.job + d.parts.k.F.job) | pct }} of the commuters' gain. The travel times measured on the timetable would share it in halves.
  • Growth is the one of the economic return. No added train, no change in where people live and work beyond what the economic return counts. A fuller RER B, D, H, L, N or P than today's would raise the value of both suburban tunnels.
