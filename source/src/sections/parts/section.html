What the suburban tunnels lack. RER F, RER G and the freed surface together return {{ d.parts.combos['F+G+S'].ratio | 2 }}. Between RER F and RER G, the gap rests on one model. Measured station to station on the timetable, the two tunnels save the same time: {{ d.times.v.plan_rer_f.hours_day | 0 }} hours a working day for RER F alone and {{ d.times.v.plan_rer_g.hours_day | 0 }} for RER G alone (see Travel times in the region). What this says about the room left. A tunnel is worth what its first line brings: the line that creates the link. Every rider of a neighbouring line then reaches it with one change, and a second line through the tunnel saves only that change. The room left in RER F and RER G is what keeps their trains on time, and what lets their lines grow: line P gained {{ d.parts.more.pub.line_p.growth_year | pct1 }} a year from {{ d.parts.more.pub.line_p.year_from | str }} to {{ d.parts.more.pub.line_p.year_to | str }}.[[pt_pest]] No published forecast was found for more trains on lines N and P.

What these figures rest on

  • The same model as the economic return, cut differently. The parts add up to the plan for every item: €{{ d.parts.combos.plan.npv | 1 }}bn of net gain and {{ d.parts.combos.plan.ratio | 2 }} for each euro. Every caution of the economic return applies to each part.
  • A part alone is not a project. The shares say where the return comes from. They do not say what the triangle would cost or bring if it were designed without the suburban tunnels: Nord-Est would be dug differently, and the surface of the terminals would stay in use.
  • The first stage's share of the gains is an assumption. It is set item by item from the lines and the trips across Paris that the first stage serves. No timetable of the first stage alone has been drawn.
  • The suburban gain is shared between RER F and RER G by one figure. The count of jobs within reach gives RER G {{ d.parts.k.G.job / (d.parts.k.G.job + d.parts.k.F.job) | pct }} of the commuters' gain. The travel times measured on the timetable would share it in halves.
  • Growth is the one of the economic return. No added train, no change in where people live and work beyond what the economic return counts. A fuller RER B, D, H, L, N or P than today's would raise the value of both suburban tunnels.
  • What else the tunnels could carry is a set of tests, not a design. The works for RER D are priced with the plan's unit prices, assumed junctions and an assumed share of works in common with RER G (see the annex on RER D). The routes of CDG Express into the RER G tunnel or the old tunnel, and its turn at Clamart, are assumptions. The gains are those of today's timetable and today's commuters, with no new traveller.
