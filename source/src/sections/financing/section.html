The money is kept in two accounts. Running the tunnels and stations would cost about €{{ f.central.ops_cost_total | m }} million a year. A charge of €{{ fp.charge_end | 2 }} on high-speed passengers who use a station their line did not serve before, €{{ fp.charge_through | 2 }} on those who cross Paris, a like charge on regional express passengers, the track charges on the new journeys and the other income found here cover {{ f.central.ops_cover | pct }} of it, and between {{ f.high_running.ops_cover | pct }} and {{ f.low_running.ops_cover | pct }} across what running costs could turn out to be. Repaying construction costs €{{ f.central.annuity | 2 }} billion a year; the surplus of operations pays €{{ f.central.cap_sources['Surplus of the operations account'] | 2 }} billion of it, and the rest has to come from taxes and grants, as for most comparable tunnels.

Two accounts

Two words first: TGV is the French high-speed train, and RER the express suburban lines that cross Paris underground. An operations account holds what it costs each year to keep the tunnels and stations open, and what the project earns each year. A capital account holds the repayment of construction, spread over 60 years like a loan, and the taxes and grants assigned to it. Keeping them apart answers two different questions: can the project pay its own way once built, and who pays to build it.

The running cost is the least certain figure

Two French records exist, and they are far apart.

  • The first RER E section (two deep stations and 4 km of tunnel, opened in 1999) cost €21.3 million a year in 2003 to maintain and operate, several times its forecast: 8.7 for maintenance of the infrastructure, 9.2 for operating the stations, 3.4 for guarding and utilities.[[cgpc]] Brought to today with the public-works index, that is €13,200 a year per metre of platform track and about €3.5 million per km of tunnel. The index is a choice: no official index exists for running costs, and consumer prices have risen less.
  • The RER network run by RATP costs more than €0.5 million per km of track a year to maintain, so about €1.0 million per km of two-track tunnel.[[fin_art24rer]]
  • Used here: stations at the RER E rate; tunnels at €{{ m.params.om_tunnel * 1000 | 2 }} million per km, midway between the two assumption. The low case takes tunnels at the RER network rate and stations at {{ fp.station_low | pct }} of the RER E rate; the high case takes both from the RER E record.
  • Gross, not net. The new stations replace terminals whose staff, cleaning and energy are paid for today. That saving is not deducted, because it has not been measured (study point I11). It would lower the cost again.
  • The level of the charge. Operations balance without any charge: with none on any passenger, income covers {{ f.no_charge.ops_cover | pct }} of running costs, {{ f.no_charge.ops_cover * f.central.ops_cost_total / f.high_running.ops_cost_total | pct }} at the high end of running costs. The charges then pay towards construction: doubling them to €{{ 2 * fp.charge_end | 2 }} and €{{ 2 * fp.charge_through | 2 }} would raise the surplus from €{{ f.central.ops_balance | m }} million to €{{ f.charge_double.ops_balance | m }} million a year, and hold back {{ f.charge_double.tgv.held | 1 }} million new journeys instead of {{ f.central.tgv.held | 1 }}.
  • The weak point is the crossing traffic. In the low traffic case, which counts passengers who cross Paris on the lower estimate, income covers {{ f.low_traffic.ops_cover | pct }} of running costs. Most of the surplus rests on the cities of two lines travelling to each other as much as the changes removed suggest (see destination by destination).
  • What the package is worth. The regional express charge, the half share of the trainset saving and the shops together bring €{{ f.central.ops_rev['Charge on regional express passengers'] + f.central.ops_rev['Share of the operators’ saving on trainsets'] + f.central.ops_rev['Shops in the new station halls'] | m }} million a year; shops at full yield would add €{{ f.shops_full.ops_rev_total - f.central.ops_rev_total | m }} million more.
  • What to ask first. The track owner and the station operator hold the real running costs of RER E, of the stations opened in 2024 and of today's terminals. They decide whether this account balances.

For comparison: fares cover 37% of the running of public transport in the Paris region (derived)[[fin_idfmbud26]] and 64% in the Zürich network.[[fin_zvv24]] The Elizabeth line earned £731 million in fares against £554 million of running costs in 2025 to 2026, trains included;[[gla]] it keeps its fares. Here the fares of high-speed trains belong to their operators and suburban fares to the region, which is why the project needs charges of its own.

A charge on high-speed passengers who use what the project adds

The proposal tested: nothing changes for a passenger who boards or leaves at the station of their own line, Gare de Lyon for the south-east, Montparnasse for the Atlantic coast, Nord-Est, entered from either historic hall, for the north and the east. Every train still stops there. A passenger who boards or leaves at another station, which their line did not serve before, pays €{{ fp.charge_end | 2 }}, the price of a metro ticket in 2026.[[fares26]] A passenger who crosses Paris pays €{{ fp.charge_through | 2 }}, on a direct train or with a change inside one station. Each passenger can avoid the charge by doing what they do today, so the charge never makes a journey dearer than it is now.

Who pays. A passenger from the Paris region uses the other station only when it saves more time than €{{ fp.charge_end | 2 }} is worth, about {{ fp.charge_end / b.vot_ld * 60 | 0 }} minutes at the official value of time: {{ f.central.tgv.pay_share | pct }} of them would, {{ f.central.tgv.paying_paris | 0 }} million journeys a year, and they save {{ f.central.tgv.gain_paris_euro / b.vot_ld * 60 | 0 }} weighted minutes on average. All {{ f.central.tgv.cross | 1 }} million journeys across Paris pay, {{ f.central.tgv.through | 1 }} million of them on a direct train.

What it brings. €{{ f.central.tgv.charge_gross * 1000 | 0 }} million a year: €{{ f.central.tgv.charge_paris * 1000 | 0 }} million from the Paris region and €{{ f.central.tgv.charge_cross * 1000 | 0 }} million from passengers who cross Paris.

What it costs in traffic. No journey made today is lost, since none pays more than today. The charge only holds back part of what the project adds. In the Paris region, some passengers keep their own station when the other would save them only a few minutes, and the new journeys drawn by the time saved fall from {{ f.no_charge.tgv.new_paris | 1 }} million to {{ f.central.tgv.new_paris | 1 }} million. Across Paris, €{{ fp.charge_through | 2 }} is {{ fp.charge_through / (2 * b.fare) | pct1 }} of two TGV fares; with French experts' response of traffic to fares, 0.7% fewer journeys for 1% more on the fare,[[cgedd_fares]] the new journeys fall from {{ f.no_charge.tgv.new_cross | 1 }} million to {{ f.central.tgv.new_cross | 1 }} million. With the charge, the project adds {{ f.central.tgv.net_new | 1 }} million high-speed journeys a year instead of {{ f.central.tgv.new_before_charge | 1 }}.

Collecting it through the operators. The charge could also be billed to the train operators as a station charge per stop at the new stations and per train crossing Paris, rather than printed on each ticket. Operators would spread it across their fares as they see fit; passengers on the most sensitive fares would then see less of it, which is the case of a response of 0.5 rather than 0.7: {{ f.resp05.tgv.net_new - f.central.tgv.net_new | 1 }} million more journeys a year. Both can be tried in the calculator.

Is it possible in law? Two routes exist. Station charges are regulated at cost plus a return: €1,260 million a year for all French stations in 2026.[[fin_art069]] The cost of the new stations would enter that base and be billed to train operators, who pass it on. European law also lets a track owner charge more for a specific project, on the basis of its long-term cost.[[fin_dir2012]] The other route is a levy written into a budget law, as for the airport express, repaid in part by a levy on air passengers.[[fin_cdgxlevy, cdgx2]] Precedents for the level: the solidarity tax on a flight from Paris within Europe is €7.40;[[fin_airtax]] operators pay the Channel Tunnel about €35 per passenger (derived, upper bound).[[fin_getlink]] A charge that depends on the station used and on crossing Paris, not on every ticket, is a matter for the legal form chosen (study point D7). The figures here take the charge as free of VAT; if it carried the 10% VAT of tickets, the project would keep €{{ fp.charge_end / 1.1 | 2 }} of each €{{ fp.charge_end | 2 }}.

A charge on regional express passengers

Regional express trains (TER) would run in the TGV tunnels, and every one of them would run through Paris. Northern TER would run through Nord-Est to Montparnasse and on to the Atlantic side, so their second station is Montparnasse; eastern TER would run through Nord-Est to Gare de Lyon and on to Burgundy, so their second station is Gare de Lyon; Atlantic TER and the TER of Gare de Lyon would run through to Nord-Est, their second station. Line R, a suburban line, stays on the surface at Gare de Lyon and pays nothing. The proposal tested: €{{ fp.rex_second | 2 }} for a passenger who gets off at the second station, the price of the metro ticket that passenger no longer buys;[[fares26]] €{{ fp.rex_through | 2 }} for a passenger who crosses Paris without getting off. Nothing for a passenger who gets off at the first station, as today.

More Navigo pass holders

Navigo is the travel pass of the Paris region. Joining the suburban lines through Paris should bring new regular users: the model expects {{ m.regional.new_transilien | 1 }} million more suburban journeys a year (see regional traffic). A pass costs €998.80 a year whatever the number of trips,[[fares26]] so a pass holder who travels more brings nothing. What counts is how many people buy a pass because of the project.

The estimate. A regular commuter makes about {{ b.pass_trips | 0 }} rail journeys a year assumption. If {{ fp.navigo_new | pct }} of the new journeys are made by new pass holders assumption, that is about {{ round(f.central.pass_holders, -2) | int }} passes and €{{ f.central.ops_rev['New Navigo pass holders'] | m }} million a year. The share is the unknown. Where it has been measured on new through lines, the share of journeys that are new to public transport was 9% to 10% in London,[[tfleval]] 27% in Sydney (derived)[[sydney]] and 28% to 32% on the East London line;[[fin_ng11]] the figure used here is at the top of that range.

How to measure it. Count passes and validations on the joined lines before and after opening, RER F (today's lines H, K and L) and RER G (today's lines P and N), and compare with lines that did not change, such as line R, which is not joined. Passes are registered to a home address, so new holders along the joined lines can be counted directly. The regional authority's budget attributes no revenue to new lines today;[[fin_idfmbud26]] an agreement would have to say that the measured gain is paid to the project.

Its weight. Small either way. Fares bring the region €4.2 billion a year,[[fin_idfmbud26]] and the project adds a few tens of millions at most. The extra running of the trains, €{{ b.om.trains * 1000 | 0 }} million a year, is charged to the project in the same account.

Growth is shown as the level yearly amount with the same value over 60 years: benefits grow at the rate chosen, passenger numbers at {{ b.traffic_growth_share | pct }} of it (the rest is the value of time rising with incomes), and running costs stay flat assumption. "Share of benefits" compares the yearly benefits of the economic return with the yearly cost of both accounts.

How comparable projects were paid for

Almost none of these tunnels was paid from its own fares. They were paid by taxes on those who gain, by passengers through a supplement, and by grants. Copenhagen is the exception worth studying.
