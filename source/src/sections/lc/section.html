What changes on the map

The TGV and TER tunnels, RER G from Nord-Est to Montparnasse through Châtelet, the underground station Nord-Est and the underground platforms at Saint-Lazare are those of the plan. Only the routes of lines H and L and of RER C change.

  • Line L and the south of RER C become one line. From its underground platforms at Saint-Lazare, line L runs {{ d.lc.km.sl_chatelet | 1 }} km to Châtelet-Les Halles, then {{ d.lc.km.chatelet_austerlitz | 1 }} km under the Seine to the RER C tracks at Gare d’Austerlitz. Its trains go on to Juvisy, Brétigny, Massy, Dourdan and Étampes. Saint-Lazare to Châtelet takes 4 minutes and Châtelet to Austerlitz 4 more, stops included assumption.
  • The west of RER C and line H become one line. The RER C trains from Versailles, Saint-Quentin-en-Yvelines and Pontoise leave their tracks at Saint-Michel Notre-Dame, run {{ d.lc.km.chatelet_saint_michel | 1 }} km to Châtelet-Les Halles and {{ d.lc.km.nord_chatelet | 1 }} km beside RER G to Nord-Est, and go on as line H. Saint-Michel to Châtelet takes 2 minutes and Châtelet to Nord-Est 4 more assumption.
  • No tunnel between Nord-Est and Saint-Lazare, and no RER C train between Saint-Michel and Austerlitz. No station loses its trains, since the two are neighbouring stops. A trip along the left bank through both, from Juvisy to Invalides for instance, changes at Châtelet.
  • Lines K and R run through as branches of RER B and RER D. Without RER F, line K has no tunnel of its own: it joins RER B, or it keeps its platforms on the surface of Gare du Nord. The variant is counted with lines K and R through, as in the annex on RER D, and compared with the plan counted the same way.
  • Above ground, the same tracks are freed. Lines H and K leave the surface of Gare du Nord and line L the surface of Saint-Lazare, as in the plan.

What the comparison says, and what it leaves open

  • On time alone, the variant comes out ahead of the plan by {{ d.lc.value.more_than_plan_kr | pct }}, for about €{{ d.lc.cost.central | 1 }}bn more. It serves more people ({{ d.times.v.variant_c.commuters_1 | 0 }} save 1 minute or more, against {{ d.times.v.plan_kb_rd.commuters_1 | 0 }}), and it is the only version tested that makes some trips slower than they were before the project.
  • Its strongest effect is on the busiest tracks of the region. It takes {{ d.lc.load.bd_relief | pct }} of the commuters out of the tunnel of RER B and D, where the plan takes {{ d.lc.load.bd_relief_plan | pct }}. The value of a less crowded RER B and D, and of the trains that could be added on the new tracks, is not counted here. It is what would decide the case.
  • The route under the Seine is not drawn. RER C runs along the left bank, just under the quays. The link from Saint-Michel has to leave it, cross under the Seine and the Île de la Cité and reach Châtelet in about a kilometre. The tunnel from Châtelet has to meet RER C under Gare d’Austerlitz. Whether both fit, and at what depth, needs a survey.
  • Châtelet-Les Halles would hold {{ d.lc.cost.tracks_chatelet_all | 0 }} new platform tracks. {{ d.lc.cost.tracks_chatelet | 0 }} for the two new lines, two for RER G and two for RER D, under the busiest interchange of the region. No comparable station has been priced; the figure here uses the plan’s price per metre of platform track.
  • Two tie-ins are made on RER C in service. Each is priced as a junction and a tie-in of the plan. The length of the closures is not estimated.
  • The trains have to run under both currents. RER C runs under 1,500 volts direct current south and west of Paris, lines H and L under 25,000 volts. The joined lines need trains for both, as RER B and D have.
  • The Pontoise branch of RER C would close a loop. It meets line H at Ermont-Eaubonne. Its trains would run from Pontoise through Invalides, Châtelet and Nord-Est and back towards Ermont-Eaubonne. The branches to pair on each side are a timetable question left open.
