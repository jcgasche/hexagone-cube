A through station is a station where trains arrive at one end and leave by the other, without turning back. A corridor is a route followed by two tunnels side by side, each with two tracks; the link has one tunnel. A portal is the place where a tunnel comes up to join an existing line. An underground junction is a cavern where two tunnels become one. The TGV is the French high-speed train. Regional express trains are the TER services that join Paris to the towns of the surrounding regions. The RER is the set of express suburban lines that cross Paris underground.

The corridors run from each station to the existing tracks at the ring road, where the tunnels come up; on the eastern line they come up at Rosa Parks, just inside it. A tunnel 35 to 40 m deep needs only about 1.1 to 1.2 km to reach the surface, and a kilometre kept underground costs far more than a kilometre of existing track, so the tunnels go no further. The three stations form a triangle: two sides of it are the corridors through Paris Nord-Est, the third is the direct link between the two southern stations. Switch layers on and off, and select a station, a corridor, a portal or a junction to read its facts.

Two networks in the tunnels

Each corridor carries two pairs of tracks, each pair in a tunnel of its own. One pair is for the main-line trains, TGVs and regional express trains together, as on the surface today. The other is for the suburban trains: lines H and K from the north run on to line N in the Atlantic, and line P from the east on to line R in the south-east. The link between Montparnasse and Gare de Lyon is one pair of tracks for TGVs only.

Cross-section of one corridor, not to scale illustrative sketch. On the RER E extension opened in 2024, one tunnel 9.6 m wide inside carries two tracks.[[eolekit]]

Why two, and not one or three

  • Suburban trains apart. They stop more often and run to a tighter rhythm. On their own pair, a late suburban train never holds up a TGV, and the pair can be signalled like a metro line.
  • TGVs and regional express trains together, as in Zürich, Vienna and Stuttgart.[[zvv, vienna, bt19]] In the stations each kind has platforms of its own, so the two share only the running tunnels.
  • The link for TGVs only. It exists for the {{ m.service_full.flows['Atlantic to southeast, by the direct link'] | 0 }} trains an hour each way between the Atlantic and the south-east when both lines are full; no regional or suburban train needs it.
  • What it adds up to. {{ b.cost_def.route_km | 1 }} km of route with two tunnels and {{ m.cost.link_km | 0 }} km with one: {{ m.cost.tunnel_km | 0 }} km of two-track tunnel, {{ m.cost.portals | 0 }} portals and {{ m.cost.junctions | 0 }} junction caverns.

Whether one suburban pair takes the whole peak of lines H, K and P is a study point: a third pair of tunnels on the two sides of the triangle would cost about €{{ (b.cost_def.route_km * (m.rates.tunnel_civil + m.rates.tunnel_systems) + b.cost_def.radials * m.rates.portal + 2 * m.rates.junction) * m.gross_up | 0 }} billion more (derived from the unit prices). The cost is built up in the cost chapter; which trains use which tracks, and how the link is used, is in trains and timetable.

Left as it is

  • The five RER lines, A to E, which already cross Paris underground.
  • The metro.
  • Saint-Lazare, Austerlitz and Bercy stations.
  • The train depots, which stay beside the approach lines; see changes at today's stations.

Not included in the cost

  • New trains.
  • Clearing the surface tracks once they are closed, and making the parks.
  • Anything built beside the parks.

How the stations sit under the present ones is in the three stations. How all this could be built while trains keep running is in works and stages.

Four parks where the tracks were

No surface platform is kept for trains. Once the deep stations are open, the four fans of tracks inside Paris are no longer needed: the two fans north of Gare du Nord and Gare de l’Est, the tracks from Gare de Lyon towards Bercy, and the tracks from Montparnasse to the ring road. The project turns them into four park areas, from the end of each station building outwards, with some small plots kept for new buildings or for reusing the buildings already there. About {{ m.params.park_ha | 0 }} hectares could be released in all, an estimate read from maps assumption; no official measure of the four track corridors exists. The one published figure found is about 30 hectares of rail corridor inside the Bercy-Charenton development area (derived).[[w_bercy]]

What stays

  • The four halls, as entrances to the stations below.
  • The RER and metro stations under them.
  • The depots, beyond the ring road, reached through the portals and the existing tracks.

What is not counted

  • The land is not sold: no income from it enters the accounts.
  • The parks are not valued: no benefit from them enters the economic return.
  • Clearing the tracks, making the parks and rebuilding on the small plots kept for building are not in the cost. Their area, value and cost are a study point; with careful planning, selling or letting those plots could pay for much of the parks assumption.

Two points are open. The airport express train to Roissy is due to end at surface platforms of Gare de l’Est;[[cdgx]] what becomes of it has not been studied. And whether Gare du Nord can be emptied at the peak depends on one suburban pair carrying the whole peak of lines H, K and P, which has not been checked against a timetable; see two networks in the tunnels.
