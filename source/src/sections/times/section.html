This chapter measures how long it takes to go from each station of the Paris region to every other station, by metro, RER, Transilien and TER, before and with the project, and counts how many people gain how much. The TGV is left out: it has its own chapters. The starting point is today’s timetable with lines 15 to 18 of the Grand Paris Express, which will be running long before the project. Every train keeps its times; the only change is that the trains of lines H, K, L, P and N and the TER run on through the new tunnels. On that cautious basis, {{ d.times.trips.over_5 | 0 }} people save 5 minutes or more on each trip to work, nobody loses, and the trips best served are cut by half: Clamart to Châtelet-Les Halles in {{ d.times.ex['Clamart>Châtelet - Les Halles'].plan | 0 }} minutes instead of {{ d.times.ex['Clamart>Châtelet - Les Halles'].today | 0 }}.

The starting point: every station, today’s times checked, lines 15 to 18 added

Every station of metro and train is in the calculation. The timetable holds {{ d.times.cover.stations | 0 }} stations: {{ d.times.cover.kinds.metro | 0 }} of the metro, {{ d.times.cover.kinds.rer | 0 }} of the RER, {{ d.times.cover.kinds.transilien | 0 }} of the Transilien lines and {{ d.times.cover.kinds.ter | 0 }} served by TER only. All of them are in the calculation, with every train that calls at them; the tramways carry travellers between them. For {{ d.times.cover.counted | 0 }} stations, Île-de-France Mobilités publishes the passengers who enter on a working day, station by station.[[idfm_valid]] The {{ d.times.cover.not_counted | 0 }} others have no count: all but one lie outside Île-de-France, at the far end of lines D, H, J, K, N, P and R and of the TER. They are in the times and weigh nothing in the averages.

Today’s times agree with a public journey planner. As a check, {{ d.times.check.n | 0 }} trips across the region were asked of a public journey planner that reads the same timetable,[[transitous]] and set beside the calculation on today’s network. On the median trip the calculation gives {{ d.times.check.median | 1 }} minutes more than the planner; {{ d.times.check.within_5 | 0 }} of the {{ d.times.check.n | 0 }} trips are within 5 minutes.

Planner: quickest trip leaving after 8:00 on Tuesday 17 November 2026, by metro, train and tramway, walks included.[[transitous]] Calculation: average of the departures between 7:30 and 8:30 on Tuesday 3 November 2026, wait for the first train included.[[idfm_tt]]

A gap of two to three minutes is what the two methods should give: the planner picks the best train of its half hour, and the calculation takes the average traveller, who also waits for the first train. Where the calculation is 5 minutes or more above the planner, the trip has two changes or few trains an hour, and the best departure is well ahead of the average one. On one trip the calculation is 8 minutes quicker: from Juvisy to Gare du Nord it rides RER D all the way, where the planner changed trains. One trip of the sample is left out: from Saint-Germain-en-Laye the planner took tramway T13, where the timetable of 3 November has RER A direct.

Lines 15 to 18 are part of the starting point. The four lines open between 2026 and 2031,[[gpe_dates]] long before the project. They are added to the timetable: {{ d.times.cover.gpe_stations | 0 }} stations, {{ d.times.cover.gpe_new | 0 }} of them where there is no station today,[[gpe_stations]] with the run times published by the Société des grands projets.[[gpe_times]] A train runs every 2.5 minutes on line 15, where 2 to 3 minutes are announced,[[gpe_l15]] and every 4 minutes on lines 16, 17 and 18 assumption. Changing between a new platform and a station within {{ d.times.k.gpe_near | 0 }} m takes {{ d.times.k.gpe_walk | 0 }} minutes plus the walk assumption.

By themselves the four lines save {{ d.times.x.gpe.hours_day | 0 }} hours a working day on the journeys to work: {{ d.times.x.gpe.commuters_1 | 0 }} people save 1 minute or more, {{ d.times.x.gpe.mean_of_gainers | 1 }} minutes on average. {{ d.times.x.gpe.od_gain[0]['from'] | str }} to {{ d.times.x.gpe.od_gain[0].to | str }} takes {{ d.times.x.gpe.od_gain[0].variant | 0 }} minutes instead of {{ d.times.x.gpe.od_gain[0].base | 0 }}.

They also take over some of the trips that the project shortens. On today’s network the project would save {{ d.times.x.plan_today.hours_day | 0 }} hours a day; once lines 15 to 18 run, it saves {{ d.times.trips.hours_day | 0 }}. Every figure of this chapter is of the second kind.

It reads a short file that says what a version of the network changes in the timetable: the new platforms, the walking times to them, the lines joined end to end and the minutes of the tunnel between them, the lines cut or run on along another line. It returns the same table of results for any version, so that two versions can be set side by side. Seven are compared here with the network before the project.

How to test another version. Copy one of the files of source/model/inputs/networks/, change it, and run three commands. The first computes the times of the version on the timetable, in under a minute; the second prints its gains against any other version; the third writes the figures of this chapter.

python3 model/regiontimes.py run model/inputs/networks/my_version.json --gtfs timetable_folder
python3 model/regiontimes.py compare future my_version
python3 model/regiontimes.py export

The code is one file, model/regiontimes.py, with the network files beside it and the result of each version already computed. The timetable itself is the open file of Île-de-France Mobilités.[[idfm_tt]]

How the times are calculated

The timetable is the real one. Every train of the metro, the RER, the Transilien lines and the tramways, and the TER trains that the regional timetable holds, on Tuesday 3 November 2026, with the walking times that Île-de-France Mobilités publishes between platforms.[[idfm_tt]] No bus and no TGV. Lines 15 to 18 are added as set out in the starting point.

The traveller sets out in the morning peak. A traveller reaches the first platform at a time taken every {{ d.times.k.step | 0 }} minutes between 7:30 and 8:30, takes the trains that arrive soonest, and changes on foot. The time of a pair of stations is the average over those starting times. The first wait is capped at {{ d.times.k.first_wait_cap | 0 }} minutes: where trains are few, people come for their train assumption.

Changes take the published walking times. Between two lines of one station where no time is published, {{ d.times.k.same_stop | 0 }} minutes; {{ d.times.k.same_stop_far | 0 }} minutes between the underground and the surface platforms of Gare du Nord and Gare de Lyon assumption. Walks between nearby stations can follow one another, up to 15 minutes.

The algorithm reads the timetable once. It is a connection scan:[[csa]] every hop of a train between two stops is read in order of departure, and can be taken by a traveller who is on that train already or stands on the platform when it leaves. All the stations and all the starting times are carried together, so that one reading gives the whole table.

With the project, every train keeps its times. A train of line H, K, L, P or N that ends in Paris today runs on through the tunnel, in the minutes of the study: 4 from Nord-Est to Saint-Lazare for RER F; 4 from Nord-Est to Châtelet-Les Halles and 9 to Montparnasse for RER G; 11 between two stations for a TER, its stop of 5 minutes included. On the other side it becomes the next train to leave, if one leaves within 10 minutes (15 for a TER), and waits underground until that train’s time. Nothing else moves, so no connection of today is lost.

On the morning studied, {{ d.times.joins[0].joined | 0 }} of the {{ d.times.joins[0].arrivals | 0 }} trains that arrive on lines H and K go on as a train of line L, after a wait of {{ d.times.joins[0].mean_wait_min | 1 }} minutes on average; {{ d.times.joins[2].joined | 0 }} of the {{ d.times.joins[2].arrivals | 0 }} trains of line P go on as a train of line N, after {{ d.times.joins[2].mean_wait_min | 1 }} minutes. The others end at the far station of the tunnel. A timetable written for the joined lines would pair every train and remove those waits: the gains of this chapter are on the low side.

The new platforms take today’s walking times. Each new platform is given the walking times of today’s suburban platforms in the same station, and {{ d.times.k.same_stop_far | 0 }} minutes to those platforms themselves; {{ d.times.k.same_stop | 0 }} minutes between two groups of new platforms in one station assumption. At Saint-Lazare, the change between line L and the other lines of the station keeps the {{ d.times.k.same_stop | 0 }} minutes of today.

The people come from counts and from the census. A station counts by the tickets and passes validated at its entrance on a working day;[[idfm_valid]] {{ d.times.k.stations | 0 }} stations enter the averages. A line counts by the boardings counted at its stations.[[sncf_count]] For the journeys to work, each town is tied to the {{ d.times.k.access_n | 0 }} nearest stations of today within {{ d.times.k.access_km | 0 }} km and to the stations of lines 15 to 18 that are as near, with the time to reach them on foot or by bus assumption, and each commuter takes the quickest pair of stations.[[mobpro]]

What is not counted.

  • Only the morning peak is calculated. Outside the peak, trains are fewer and each change costs more time; that case is not calculated.
  • No train is added and no timetable is rewritten: the trains of today run through. More trains, or trains timed for the changes, would add to the gains.
  • Buses are left out, so a trip that is quicker by bus today is counted by rail.
  • The TER trains from outside Île-de-France are only partly in the regional timetable. Their passengers are studied in crossing Paris.
  • Everyone takes the quickest route. Where two routes are close, the calculation sends all the travellers by one of them.
  • The homes and the jobs are those of the census of 2022. Lines 15 to 18 will move some of them; that is not counted.
  • Room on the trains and delays are not counted here, and only journeys to work are weighed, not the trips for study, shopping or leisure.
  • The figures of this chapter are not carried into the economic return, which keeps its own estimate of the benefits for the region.
