Enough detail to check the figures or to redo them with better data. Everything is computed by a small open model: Python scripts whose results feed every number in this document, and the same arithmetic in the browser for the calculators.

The access model

  • Points: 1,000 points on an even grid over Paris, 315 m apart. For the suburbs, the centre of each of 1,265 communes, weighted by population.
  • Network: every metro, RER and tram line, and the suburban train lines for the communes outside Paris, with stations, stop order and published interchange times, from the data of Île-de-France Mobilités of 5 October 2026.[[metropolist]] Buses are not included.
  • Speeds: walking at 4.5 km/h on a path 30% longer than the straight line; each metro line at its own average speed (20 to 28.5 km/h); RER at 60 km/h plus one minute per stop. Waiting is half the interval between trains. In the suburbs, up to 1 km on foot to a station, further at 20 km/h.
  • Result: the quickest time from each point to the hall of each of the four terminals, and to the Nord-Est station taken as the nearer of its two halls.
  • Saint-Lazare is derived, not measured. The network data were not run for Saint-Lazare. For each point and each weighting, a straight line, time = a + b × distance, is fitted on the point's times to Gare du Nord, Gare de l'Est and Montparnasse and applied to its distance to Saint-Lazare.

    The result is never taken below 6 minutes plus 2.5 minutes a km (a metro at about 24 km/h with access and wait, scaled for the weighting), and walking is used when it is shorter assumption. These times feed only the regional benefits of RER F; they are to be replaced by a run of the network model (study point I22).

  • The triangle's rules: every TGV stops at two of the three stations and no train reverses. Trains to the north and the east stop at Nord-Est and at one southern station; trains between the Atlantic and the south-east stop at Montparnasse and Gare de Lyon by the direct link. Each line keeps its home station, so for each direction the passenger boards at the home station or at the train's other Paris stop.
  • Two measures. The plain one is the time to the entrance of the nearest station where a train of the line stops, the two halves of the trains of each line averaged ({{ m.paris.today | 1 }} minutes on the clock today from Paris, {{ pts.avg.station | 1 }} with the project). It is the one used to count the benefits of TGV passengers: on a long trip the minutes spent seated on the right train across Paris matter little assumption.

    The second adds the ride the train then makes between its two Paris stops when the passenger boards at the far one: the {{ m.TH_ACCESS.L | 0 }}-minute run between any two of the three stations, the passenger boarding a train already standing at the platform assumption. Crossing Paris keeps the run and the stop, {{ m.TH.L | 0 }} minutes ({{ m.run_min | 0 }} and {{ m.stop_min | 0 }}), the beat of the timetable.

    The second measure supposes that the passenger picks the train of the line that calls at their best station, half of the trains of each line, without a longer wait; it gives {{ m.paris.ride | 1 }} minutes and is shown on the map and as a case in the accounts. Regional and suburban passengers are counted this way, with their own rides: {{ m.TR.L | 0 }} minutes from Nord-Est to Montparnasse, the same run and a 1-minute stop, and {{ m.TR.SL | 0 }} minutes from Nord-Est to Saint-Lazare on RER F assumption.

  • Passenger choice. The central case assumes that passengers take the first train of their line and so get the ride between stops half of the time; the choice measure assumes that they plan around it. The truth lies between the two and depends on frequency and on how tickets are sold assumption (study point I16).
  • Never longer than today: each train still stops where it does now, so no point can lose on either measure; {{ m.share_quicker | pct }} of the Paris points gain and the rest are unchanged. The export script stops with an error if a point loses.
  • Time inside the station is not in these figures. It is compared separately (see inside the station) and tested as two extra minutes for everyone (see the tests).
  • Weights: one run on the clock; one with the official weights on walking, waiting and changing; one with metro and RER riding counted 25% heavier for crowding.

Cost

  • Build-up. Quantities times unit prices from final costs and signed contracts, which already hold past overruns; the prices, their sources and the test against seven projects are in the cost chapter. Works €{{ m.cost.hard | 1 }}bn, then {{ (m.rates.design + m.rates.client) | pct }} for design and client costs and {{ m.rates.land | pct }} for land bought, applied to the works; then {{ m.rates.risk | pct }} for risk on the whole, €{{ m.cost.total | 1 }}bn in all (gross-up {{ m.gross_up | 3 }}).
  • Why 15%. With prices that come from outturns rather than estimates, the allowance covers what is specific to this project and not the general drift of projects, which the prices already contain. The back-test against seven built projects gives the model with 15% a result within the range of the published costs (see the back-test). {{ m.rates_low.risk | pct }} with low prices and {{ m.rates_high.risk | pct }} with high prices bound the range: €{{ m.cost_low | 1 }}bn to €{{ m.cost_high | 1 }}bn. The two accounts, the return and taxes

  • Yearly repayment. Construction cost, increased by interest during {{ m.params.build_years | 0 }} years of even spending, repaid in equal yearly amounts over 60 years at 3% above inflation: each €1 billion costs €{{ m.AF * m.idc * 1000 | 0 }} million a year.
  • Operations account. Costs: running the stations at the rate recorded on the first RER E section (€13,200 a year per metre of platform track) and the tunnels at €{{ m.params.om_tunnel * 1000 | 2 }} million per km, midway between that record (€3.5 million) and the average of the RER network (€1.0 million); one renewal of the tunnels' rail systems after 30 years, set aside evenly; extra running of regional trains.

    What today's terminals cost to run is not deducted (study point I11). Income: the TGV charge, the regional express charge, track charges on the new high-speed journeys (two fares for a journey across Paris), new Navigo passes, regional express fares, the authority's fee, shop rents, and half of the operators' saving on trainsets.

  • The TGV charge. It falls only on new uses. A Paris-region passenger pays c for boarding or leaving at a station their line did not serve, and chooses it only when the minutes saved, at €{{ b.vot_ld | 0 }} an hour, are worth more than c; otherwise they keep their own station at today's price. That choice is made point by point on the access map, and the new journeys follow the time model on the minutes saved less the charge.

    A passenger who crosses Paris pays 2c; those journeys are multiplied by (1 + 2c / two fares) to the power {{ b.fare_response | 1 }}, with a fare of €{{ b.fare | 2 }} before VAT.[[cgedd_fares]] A response of {{ fp.fare_response_low | 1 }} is shown beside the central one. Track charges are counted on the new journeys, two fares for a crossing assumption. No VAT is counted on the charges.

  • The regional express charge. €{{ fp.rex_second | 2 }} for each passenger who gets off at the second station ({{ b.traffic.central.rex_second | 1 }} million a year) and €{{ fp.rex_through | 2 }} for each who crosses Paris without stopping ({{ b.traffic.central.rex_through | 1 }} million), with no loss of journeys counted assumption. Nothing on suburban trains: the Navigo pass and the Île-de-France ticket already cover the ride.
  • Trainsets. With no train waiting in a terminal, TGV operators need {{ m.extras.fleet_sets_low | 0 }} to {{ m.extras.fleet_sets_high | 0 }} fewer sets at €{{ b.set_year | m }} million a set a year; {{ fp.fleet_capture | pct }} of the midpoint is counted as income in the central case assumption. Shops are counted at half of the Montparnasse yield; full yield is an option shown in the financing chapter.
  • Navigo. New suburban journeys, times the share made by new pass holders, divided by {{ b.pass_trips | 0 }} journeys a year per commuter, times the price of a yearly pass.
  • Capital account. The yearly repayment plus any shortfall of operations, less a tax on businesses of the size of the Grand Paris Express one and a European grant; what is left is expressed as a grant paid during the works. No land is sold in the capital account; the parks and the building plots on the terminal sites are valued and costed in the economic return (study point I19).
  • Economic return. Two readings of one list of benefits. The simple reading divides the benefits of the opening year at today's traffic by the yearly cost at 3% over 60 years: passengers' time, the railway's own gains (operators' margin and track charges net of wear), comfort and the regular timetable, job access across Paris, the environment and the parks; new journeys count for half of the gain, and those the charge holds back for none.

    The official reading applies the French rules: a 4.5% rate, long-distance traffic growing {{ d.french.params.ld_growth | pct1 }} a year and values of time {{ d.french.params.vot_growth | pct1 }} to {{ d.french.params.last | str }}, a residual value to {{ d.french.params.resid_end | str }}, each euro of public money at {{ d.french.params.cofp | 2 }}, wider effects reported apart; its headline is a net present value.

    A second ratio divides the benefits by the cost net of the project's income, the basis of British appraisals. The rate of return is the discount rate at which the value is nil.

  • What public budgets get back. VAT at 10% inside the net change in ticket money, less fuel duty on car trips moved to rail. Then three readings of the added activity: none of it is new for the country; or it is counted as in the appraisal the ratios come from (each job worth €10,000 a year of benefits saved, output from denser job markets and from working time saved on the clock taxed at 43.6%); or every job implied is new and taxed on the average output of a job in the region.

The scripts are in the model folder of the repository: nordest.py (access, traffic, cost), finance.py (accounts, return, taxes), export.py (data for this site). src/js/model.js repeats the accounts in the browser and is checked against the Python results to fifteen digits.

What has been checked, and what has not

  • Checked by an independent reviewer, on an earlier layout of the project: the access model, the traffic estimates, the construction cost, the two accounts, the passenger charge, the Navigo estimate, what public budgets get back and the comparison with other projects. The figures of the present layout have not been checked again.
  • Not yet reviewed by anyone but their authors: the layout as it stands, with the triangle and its direct link, the tunnels by kind of train, the station under Saint-Lazare, the regional express trains in the TGV tunnels with their paths, loads and links, and the fourth track each way at Gare de Lyon; the platform rules, the regional express charge, the trainset saving, the parks, the second access measure and the derived Saint-Lazare times; and the full count of benefits in the economic return. The study points I1 to I27 listed in risks and next steps are open.
  • Not checked against outside sources by anyone but their authors: the three annexes on Zürich, the Elizabeth line and the Swiss timetable, and the drawings of stations and works, which are sketches.
  • Access times are times to the entrance of a station for TGV passengers, plus the ride between stops for regional and suburban passengers. No minutes are added for the depth of platforms; time inside the station is treated separately.
