Enough detail to check the figures or to redo them with better data. Everything is computed by a small open model: Python scripts whose results feed every number in this document, and the same arithmetic in the browser for the calculators. The model was rebuilt for the triangle on 13 October 2026.

The access model

  • Points: 1,000 points on an even grid over Paris, 315 m apart. For the suburbs, the centre of each of 1,265 communes, weighted by population.
  • Network: every metro, RER and tram line, and the suburban train lines for the communes outside Paris, with stations, stop order and published interchange times, from the data of Île-de-France Mobilités of 5 October 2026.[[metropolist]] Buses are not included.
  • Speeds: walking at 4.5 km/h on a path 30% longer than the straight line; each metro line at its own average speed (20 to 28.5 km/h); RER at 60 km/h plus one minute per stop. Waiting is half the interval between trains. In the suburbs, up to 1 km on foot to a station, further at 20 km/h.
  • Result: the quickest time from each point to the hall of each of the four terminals, and to the Nord-Est station taken as the nearer of its two halls.
  • The triangle's rules: every TGV stops at two of the three stations and no train reverses. Trains to the north and the east stop at Nord-Est and at one southern station; trains between the Atlantic and the south-east stop at Montparnasse and Gare de Lyon by the direct link. Each line keeps its home station, so for each direction the passenger boards at the home station or at the train's other Paris stop.
  • Two measures. The plain one is the time to the entrance of the nearest station where a train of the line stops, the two halves of the trains of each line averaged ({{ m.paris.today | 1 }} weighted minutes today from Paris, {{ pts.avg.station | 1 }} with the project). It is the one used to count the benefits of TGV passengers: on a long trip the minutes spent seated on the right train across Paris matter little assumption. The second adds the ride the train then makes between its two Paris stops when the passenger boards at the far one: {{ m.TH.L | 0 }} minutes from Nord-Est to Gare de Lyon, {{ m.TH.M | 0 }} to Montparnasse and {{ m.TH.LM | 0 }} between Montparnasse and Gare de Lyon, stop included assumption, and supposes that the passenger picks the train of the line that calls at their best station, half of the trains of each line, without a longer wait; it gives {{ m.paris.ride | 1 }} minutes and is shown on the map and as a case in the accounts. Regional and suburban passengers are counted this way.
  • Passenger choice. The central case assumes that passengers take the first train of their line and so get the ride between stops half of the time; the choice measure assumes that they plan around it. The truth lies between the two and depends on frequency and on how tickets are sold assumption (study point I16).
  • Never longer than today: each train still stops where it does now, so no point can lose on either measure; {{ m.share_quicker | pct }} of the Paris points gain and the rest are unchanged. The export script stops with an error if a point loses.
  • Time inside the station is not in these figures. It is compared separately (see inside the station) and tested as two extra minutes for everyone (see the tests).
  • Weights: one run on the clock; one with the official weights on walking, waiting and changing; one with metro and RER riding counted 25% heavier for crowding.

New passengers are valued at half the time saved, as in every transport appraisal: they gain less than those who already travelled. Yearly figures mix sources that turn a weekday count into a year with factors from 273 to 435, and base years from 2012 to 2025; they are orders of magnitude, not counts. Benefits are held flat over time in the central case; growth of {{ fp.growth | pct1 }} a year is a separate case (study point I17).

Official rules that are not applied. A full French appraisal would let the value of time rise with income, discount at 3.2% to 4.6% instead of 3%,[[fsrate]] and count each euro of public money as 1.2 euros.[[dgitm]] The first helps the project and the other two count against it. The central case applies none of them; growth of benefits and the two discount rates are tested in the tests, and growth is shown beside the flat case in the economic return.

Cost

  • Build-up. Quantities times unit prices from final costs and signed contracts, which already hold past overruns; the prices, their sources and the test against seven projects are in the cost chapter. Works €{{ m.cost.hard | 1 }}bn, then {{ (m.rates.design + m.rates.client) | pct }} for design and client costs and {{ m.rates.land | pct }} for land bought, applied to the works; then {{ m.rates.risk | pct }} for risk on the whole, €{{ m.cost.total | 1 }}bn in all (gross-up {{ m.gross_up | 3 }}).
  • Why 15%. With prices that come from outturns rather than estimates, the allowance covers what is specific to this project and not the general drift of projects, which the prices already contain. The back-test against seven built projects gives the model with 15% a result within the range of the published costs (see the back-test). {{ m.rates_low.risk | pct }} with low prices and {{ m.rates_high.risk | pct }} with high prices bound the range: €{{ m.cost_low | 1 }}bn to €{{ m.cost_high | 1 }}bn. Whether reviewers accept 15% is study point I10.
  • The link and the London hall. The direct TGV link between Montparnasse and Gare de Lyon is {{ m.cost.link_km | 0 }} km at the tunnel price; the control hall and waiting room for London departures above Nord-Est are €{{ m.rates.london_hall | 1 }}bn assumption. Six junctions at €{{ m.rates.junction | 2 }}bn and {{ m.cost.portals | 0 }} portals at €{{ m.rates.portal | 2 }}bn are priced as items.
  • Platforms by direction. TGV platforms are dedicated by direction, {{ m.tracks_per_direction | 0 }} tracks each way at each station. A stop takes {{ m.stop_min | 0 }} minutes, a track needs {{ m.clear_min | 0 }} minutes between a departure and the next arrival, and a timetable can use {{ m.util | pct }} of that cycle, so one track takes {{ m.per_track | 1 }} trains an hour; with the lines full the load is {{ m.service_full.nordest_load | 1 }} at Nord-Est, {{ m.service_full.lyon_load | 1 }} at Gare de Lyon and {{ m.service_full.montparnasse_load | 1 }} at Montparnasse. The three figures of the rule are assumptions (see platforms). Regional express platforms are {{ m.len.ter | 0 }} m, 14 in all; suburban platforms {{ m.len.sub | 0 }} m.

The two accounts, the return and taxes

  • Yearly repayment. Construction cost, increased by interest during {{ m.params.build_years | 0 }} years of even spending, repaid in equal yearly amounts over 60 years at 3% above inflation: each €1 billion costs €{{ m.AF * m.idc * 1000 | 0 }} million a year.
  • Operations account. Costs: running the stations at the rate recorded on the first RER E section (€13,200 a year per metre of platform track) and the tunnels at €{{ m.params.om_tunnel * 1000 | 2 }} million per km, midway between that record (€3.5 million) and the average of the RER network (€1.0 million); one renewal of the tunnels' rail systems after 30 years, set aside evenly; extra running of regional trains. What today's terminals cost to run is not deducted (study point I11). Income: the TGV charge, the regional express charge, track charges on the net change in high-speed journeys, new Navigo passes, regional express fares, the authority's fee, shop rents, and half of the operators' saving on trainsets.
  • The TGV charge. Journeys fall by the fare response: a charge of c on a fare of €{{ b.fare | 2 }} keeps (1 + c / fare) to the power {{ b.fare_response | 1 }} of the journeys.[[cgedd_fares]] The fare is the average revenue per journey before VAT, and the same fare is used for passengers who cross Paris; both overstate the loss. A response of {{ fp.fare_response_low | 1 }} is shown beside the central one. Track charges are taken to follow the net change in journeys assumption. No VAT is counted on the charges.
  • The regional express charge. €{{ fp.rex_second | 2 }} for each passenger who gets off at the second station ({{ b.traffic.central.rex_second | 1 }} million a year) and €{{ fp.rex_through | 2 }} for each who crosses Paris without stopping ({{ b.traffic.central.rex_through | 1 }} million), with no loss of journeys counted assumption. Nothing on suburban trains: the Navigo pass and the Île-de-France ticket already cover the ride.
  • Trainsets. With no train waiting in a terminal, operators need {{ m.extras.fleet_sets_low | 0 }} to {{ m.extras.fleet_sets_high | 0 }} fewer sets at €{{ b.set_year | m }} million a set a year; {{ fp.fleet_capture | pct }} of the midpoint is counted as income in the central case assumption. Shops are counted at half of the Montparnasse yield; full yield is an option shown in the financing chapter.
  • Navigo. New suburban journeys, times the share made by new pass holders, divided by {{ b.pass_trips | 0 }} journeys a year per commuter, times the price of a yearly pass.
  • Capital account. The yearly repayment plus any shortfall of operations, less a tax on businesses of the size of the Grand Paris Express one and a European grant; what is left is expressed as a grant paid during the works. No land is sold: the terminal sites become parks, which are neither valued nor costed (study point I19).
  • Economic return. Yearly benefits divided by yearly cost, at 3%; the same at 4.5% is shown beside it. Benefits are the time saved, comfort and wider effects, less the value of the journeys given up because of the charges. The ticket money of train operators is not counted. A second ratio divides the benefits by the cost net of the project's income, the basis of British appraisals. With benefits growing g a year, the level yearly amount of the same value over 60 years is used; passenger numbers, and so the charges and the shops, grow at {{ b.traffic_growth_share | pct }} of g, and running costs stay flat assumption. The rate of return is the discount rate at which 60 years of benefits less running costs equal the construction cost.
  • What public budgets get back. VAT at 10% inside the net change in ticket money, less fuel duty on car trips moved to rail. Then three readings of the added activity: none of it is new for the country; or it is counted as in the appraisal the ratios come from (each job worth €10,000 a year of benefits saved, output from denser job markets and from working time saved on the clock taxed at 43.6%); or every job implied is new and taxed on the average output of a job in the region.

The scripts are in the model folder of the repository: nordest.py (access, traffic, cost), finance.py (accounts, return, taxes), export.py (data for this site). src/js/model.js repeats the accounts in the browser and is checked against the Python results to fifteen digits.

What has been checked, and what has not

  • Checked twice by an independent reviewer, for the earlier layouts: the access model, the traffic estimates and the construction cost.
  • Checked once, on 12 October 2026, and corrected, for the earlier layouts: the two accounts, the passenger charge, the Navigo estimate, what public budgets get back and the comparison with other projects. The reviewer's main corrections were to the running cost of tunnels, now set between two French records where the first version used the higher one; to the tax estimate, now given as three readings; and to the comparison with other projects, whose bases differ.
  • Not yet reviewed: the rebuild of the model and of this document for the triangle on 13 October 2026. The two earlier reviews covered the previous layouts; the direct link, the platform rule by direction, the regional express charge, the trainset saving, the parks, the second access measure and the shorter approach tunnels, with portals at the ring road, have not been checked by anyone but their authors. The study points I1 to I20 listed in risks and next steps are open.
  • Not checked against outside sources by anyone but their authors: the three annexes on Zürich, the Elizabeth line and the Swiss timetable, and the drawings of stations and works, which are sketches.
  • Access times are times to the entrance of a station for TGV passengers, plus the ride between stops for regional and suburban passengers. No minutes are added for the depth of platforms; time inside the station is treated separately.
