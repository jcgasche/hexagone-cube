A passenger who crosses Paris today spends about an hour between two trains, with a metro ride in the middle. With the stations joined, five of the six pairs of lines get direct trains and none of them reverses: {{ m.connecting.central.pairs['Montparnasse-Lyon'].direct | 0 }} minutes from Montparnasse to Gare de Lyon by the direct link, {{ m.TH.L | 0 }} or {{ m.TH.M | 0 }} minutes through Nord-Est for the other pairs, and a change of train happens on the platforms of one station. Suburban and regional passengers gain less on each trip, {{ m.regional.avg_clock_saving | 1 }} minutes on the clock on average, but there are {{ m.regional.users | 0 }} million such journeys a year.

TGV passengers who cross Paris

About {{ b.traffic.central.paris_base + b.traffic.central.cross_base | 0 }} million journeys a year by TGV, the French high-speed train, touch Paris: {{ b.traffic.central.paris_base | 0 }} million start or end there and {{ b.traffic.central.cross_base | 1 }} million cross it, changing between two Paris stations, usually by metro or by RER, the express suburban lines that cross Paris underground. The market the count misses

The survey counts people who already cross Paris by train. It does not count those who go by car or by air between the regions because of the change of station, nor those who take the trains that go round Paris on the bypass line, with stops at Marne-la-Vallée, Roissy and Massy: 21.9 million passengers in 2009, 17.6 million of them on trains through those outer stations.[[interco_sud]] The example below shows why many of them would rather go through: for the pairs that include the Atlantic line, the trains round Paris are slow and few. Regional and suburban passengers

Suburban lines H, K, P, N and R and the regional express trains end at the four terminals today, unlike the RER. Run through, each train stops at a second station, and passengers stay on board when that station is nearer to where they are going. Suburban passengers pay nothing more for it: the Navigo pass and the Île-de-France ticket already cover the metro ride they no longer make.

Lines H and K from the north run on to Gare de Lyon and join line R. Line P from the east runs on to Montparnasse and joins line N. {{ m.regional.second_station_sub | 1 }} million suburban passengers a year would use the second station (derived from the access model: {{ m.regional.second_station_share.H | pct }} of line H passengers, {{ m.regional.second_station_share.R | pct }} of line R). Regional express trains do the same: those from the north and east continue to Gare de Lyon or Montparnasse, and a passenger takes the one that goes to the station that suits them assumption; those from the south continue to Nord-Est. {{ m.regional.second_station_ter | 1 }} million of their passengers a year would get off at the second station, and {{ m.regional.through_ter | 1 }} million would cross Paris without leaving the train, taken as {{ m.params.ter_through_share | pct }} of today's passengers plus the gain from the change removed assumption; a count of them is study point I15.

Trains are assumed to take {{ m.TR.L | 0 }} to {{ m.TR.M | 0 }} minutes between two stations assumption. The saving for each passenger is the difference between the trip from the old terminal to their destination in Paris and the trip from whichever of the two stops is nearer, measured on the public transport network as in reaching the station.

Why two minutes matter

  • Fewer people change onto the metro and the RER at the terminals. Gare du Nord and Gare de l'Est together see 800,000 travellers a day.[[nordest]] A commuter from the northern suburbs who works near Gare de Lyon no longer goes down to RER D at Gare du Nord: the train goes there.
  • Crowding counts. In a survey of Île-de-France passengers, a minute in a full metro weighed up to 1.39 times a minute in an uncrowded one when seated, and 1.55 when standing.[[kroes]] The estimate carries the comfort gained at €{{ m.yearly.comfort | m }} million a year, in the proportion found in the appraisal of the Grand Paris Express.[[sgpi]] The relief of each metro line is not modelled.
  • Not counted: the relief of the crowded metro lines, more frequent trains if the lines are rebuilt as through lines, and trips from one suburb to another across Paris, which become direct. They need the region's travel model: study point I14. The traffic these lines could gain is in how many more passengers.
