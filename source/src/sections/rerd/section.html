This annex tests an addition to the plan. RER B and RER D share one tunnel between Gare du Nord and Châtelet-Les Halles, the busiest pair of tracks in the region. The plan digs the RER G tunnel right beside it. If the same works gave RER D a pair of tracks of its own, the two lines would stop holding each other up. It would cost about €{{ d.rerd.cost.own.central | 1 }}bn to €{{ d.rerd.cost.moved.central | 1 }}bn on top of the plan. With the trains it makes room for, it returns {{ d.parts.rerd.cases.growth.french.ratio | 2 }} for each euro of cost by the rules of the economic return. It is not in the plan, nor in its cost or its benefits.

Why look at RER D

  • One tunnel for two of the busiest lines. Between Châtelet and Gare du Nord, RER B and RER D carry about {{ d.rerd.k.bd | 0 }} passengers a day on two tracks: {{ d.rerd.k.b_bd | 0 }} on RER B and {{ d.rerd.k.d_bd | 0 }} on RER D (see the map of passengers by line).
  • The timetable asks for more than the tunnel gives. It holds 20 RER B and 12 RER D trains an hour each way, 32 in all.[[idfm_b22]] About 28 to 29 run in practice,[[cc_rer]] and RER B runs 17.5 of its 20.[[rd_b23]] A late train of one line delays the other.
  • The new signalling does not add trains. NExTEO, due on this section by 2032, is expected to let about 31 of the 32 trains through. The limit of 32 stays.[[rd_ce]]
  • The plan builds a tunnel beside it for far fewer passengers. The RER G tunnel would carry about {{ d.rerd.k.g | 0 }} passengers a day between Nord-Est and Châtelet, for {{ d.flows.k.h.g | 0 }} trains an hour where its platforms take {{ d.flows.sec.g.cap | 0 }}. RER D carries {{ d.rerd.k.times | 1 }} times as many through the old tunnel next to it.

The works of RER G open the ground between the two stations once: shafts, a cavern at Châtelet, a station at Nord-Est. This annex asks what it would take to bring RER D through them as well, and whether the price holds against the studies made in 2003 and 2013.

What it would bring

The value has two parts, and both come from the new pair of tracks.

  • Fewer delays, at today's service. With each line alone the section adds {{ d.rerd.sim.today.six.saved_min | 0 }} fewer minutes of delay a working day, over the passengers of the three lines. At 2 for the weight of a late minute, that is about €{{ d.rerd.value.own.reliability * 1000 | 0 }}m a year ({{ d.rerd.value.own.reliability_low * 1000 | 0 }} to {{ d.rerd.value.own.reliability_high * 1000 | 0 }}).
  • Room for trains that cannot run today. The shared tunnel holds RER B at 20 trains an hour and RER D at 12. The official forecasts of 2014 found no need for more than 12 RER D trains in the north,[[rd_stif14]] on a line that now has the fullest trains of the Transilien network.[[idfm_d]] With its own pair RER D can run 16 to 20, and RER B alone can run 24. What the long branches to Crépy-en-Valois, Montereau and Montargis, with few trains an hour, would do to the punctuality of the two RER lines. The simulation of this annex takes four more trains on RER B and on RER D as trains like the others.

What would settle it

  • The study of 2012-13 itself. Its four routes, their depths, the position of the stations and the detail of the 1.3 to 3.8 billion euros are not published. Île-de-France Mobilités ordered it; SNCF Réseau, the RATP and Setec took part.
  • The RATP study of 2003, for the tie-in at Châtelet and the length of the closure it implied.
  • Where delays start. The minutes of delay and the trains cancelled on RER B and RER D, by cause and by place, would replace the simulation with a measurement.
  • Loads at the peak on RER B and RER D north of Gare du Nord, to say how many more trains are needed and when.
  • A view from those who priced the tunnel: whether new platforms in the caverns of the plan remove the difficulties found in 2003 and 2013, and what a tie-in to the RER D tunnel of 1995 would cost.

These questions join the list of what is still to do.
