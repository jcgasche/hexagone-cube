The case rests on a handful of things nobody has yet measured or drawn. Cost and the time needed to board in a deep station move the economic result most; the level of running costs decides whether the project can pay its own way once open. Most of these can be settled by studies that cost little next to the project.

Stages and the decision

The project can be built in two stages: first the north to southeast axis with Paris Nord-Est and Gare de Lyon (€{{ m.stages.first.total | 0 }} billion for layout A), then Montparnasse and the Atlantic (€{{ m.stages.second.total | 0 }} billion); see works and stages. The first stage serves the trains of three of the four high-speed lines. Its benefits on its own have not been estimated, so this study cannot say whether stopping after it would be a better or a worse project.

What to decide now

Not whether to build. The decision today is whether to spend on five quick tests that can confirm or stop the case:

  1. Ask for ticket data on passengers who change station in Paris, by origin and destination (study point C1).
  2. Draw one standard hour of trains with the track owner (SNCF Réseau), including trains that reverse at Nord-Est (A1, A2).
  3. Commission a concept design of the Nord-Est station: position, depth, time from hall to train, and a first engineer's price (A4, B2).
  4. Ask what deep stations and tunnels cost to run, from the records of RER E and of the stations opened in 2024 (B5). Whether the project can pay its own running costs turns on it.
  5. Ask when each terminal and each line is full, with and without the project (C6). The economic case turns on it, and it is not valued in this study.

With those in hand, choose between layouts A and B (A7), settle the level and the legal form of the passenger charge (D7), and take the case to a full appraisal.

The case in three sentences
  • It serves passengers better than today, and nobody is further from a train than before.
  • It does not pay for itself. With a charge of €{{ fp.charge_end | 2 }} on each high-speed passenger, income covers {{ f.A_central.ops_cover | pct }} to {{ f.B_central.ops_cover | pct }} of running costs, perhaps all of them for layout B, and none of the construction; the benefits counted are worth {{ f.A_central.econ_cover_all | pct }} to {{ f.B_central.econ_cover_all | pct }} of the yearly cost.
  • It is a public investment, to be judged like the Grand Paris Express: on room for the next century's trains and on the city it leaves behind. Those are the benefits this study could not count, and the case stands or falls on them. It is strongest for layout B.
