The stations, not the tunnels, are the largest item.

The prices used, and where they come from

  1. Quantities come from the layout: kilometres of two-track tunnel and metres of platform track.
  2. Unit prices come from built projects, brought to 2026 euros with the French public-works index.[[insee]] That index follows contractors' costs. Prices for underground work around Paris have risen faster: the Grand Paris Express by about 15 points more between 2012 and 2020 (derived).[[senat44]] Older projects are therefore understated.
  3. Design and client costs add 20%, the SNCF convention at this stage (12% and 8%).[[melun]] Land adds 7%; 5% to 10% is usual.[[cdc24]]
  4. Risk adds 30% on top. The Grand Paris Express carries 24% to 32% since 2017.[[senat44]] The RER E extension carried 6% and ended 45% over budget.[[d92over]]

Why €1.0 million per metre. The evidence from Paris points two ways. The first RER E stations, Saint-Lazare and Magenta, have four tracks each in caverns 30 m down, like the ones needed here. The whole 1999 line cost €2.5 billion in today's money; with its 4 km of tunnel deducted at our price, the stations come to about €0.9 million per metre, everything included (derived).[[cgpc]] The two-track stations opened in 2024 cost €0.9 million to €1.4 million per metre for their structure alone, before fit-out.[[eolekit, d92cnit]] The price used sits between them: it leans high against the one complete Paris figure and low against London. With design, client costs, land and risk, a metre of platform track is carried at €{{ m.gross_up * m.rates.platform_m * 1000 | 2 }} million.

Not priced separately: the caverns at each end of a station where two tracks fan out into many. They are taken as covered by the station price, as in a French study of a four-track deep station whose box is 938 m long for 365 m of platform.[[lyon15]] For a station of {{ m.cost.A.tracks['Paris Nord-Est'] | 0 }} tracks this is untested.

Result

Does the method hold?

The same prices were applied to seven projects that have been built or priced, and compared with their published cost.

  • Paris, built. The prices overshoot the first RER E section ({{ m.backtest[0].model_no_risk | 1 }} against {{ m.backtest[0].actual | 1 }} before risk) and undershoot the 2024 extension ({{ m.backtest[1].model_no_risk | 1 }} against {{ m.backtest[1].actual | 1 }} to {{ m.backtest[1].actual_high | 1 }}). With the 30% allowance the extension is matched at the low reading of its cost and still missed at the high one.
  • France, study estimates. Before risk, the prices come close to what SNCF Réseau itself estimates for a deep four-track station with its tunnels: {{ m.backtest[2].model_no_risk | 1 }} against {{ m.backtest[2].actual | 1 }} for Lyon, {{ m.backtest[3].model_no_risk | 1 }} against {{ m.backtest[3].actual | 1 }} for Marseille, a figure that covers a whole phase of works. Those estimates carry a contingency of their own, and in Paris such estimates have run 45% over.
  • Abroad. Stuttgart falls inside the range ({{ m.backtest[4].model_no_risk | 1 }} to {{ m.backtest[4].model_with_risk | 1 }} against {{ m.backtest[4].actual | 1 }}, which is in money of each year and would be higher in 2026 euros). Zürich cost much less than the prices give. Munich, with stations 40 m under the centre, is costing twice as much.
The reading

The prices are those of a French study estimate, and the 30% is what it takes to reach what such projects end up costing. It is not a cushion. The spread between Zürich and Munich is the real uncertainty.

The first RER E section cost €21.3 million a year in 2003 to maintain and operate, for two deep stations and 4 km of tunnel; maintenance of the structures alone was six times the forecast.[[cgpc]] Brought to today with the public-works index, that is €13,200 a year per metre of platform track and about €3.5 million per km of tunnel. The RER network run by RATP costs more than €0.5 million per km of track to maintain, about €1.0 million per km of two-track tunnel.[[fin_art24rer]] Stations are taken at the first figure and tunnels midway between the two, €{{ m.params.om_tunnel * 1000 | 2 }} million per km assumption. The range, and what it does to the accounts, is in the operations account.

The State borrows long-term at 5.4% in October 2026, inflation included.[[aft]] The saving from closing the surface platforms and tracks is not deducted. Running high-speed trains is left to their operators.
