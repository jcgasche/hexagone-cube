Enough detail to check the figures or to redo them with better data. Everything is computed by a small open model: Python scripts whose results feed every number in this document, and the same arithmetic in the browser for the calculators.

The access model

  • Points: 1,000 points on an even grid over Paris, 315 m apart. For the suburbs, the centre of each of 1,265 communes, weighted by population.
  • Network: every metro, RER and tram line, and the suburban train lines for the communes outside Paris, with stations, stop order and published interchange times, from the data of Île-de-France Mobilités of 5 October 2026.[[metropolist]] Buses are not included.
  • Speeds: walking at 4.5 km/h on a path 30% longer than the straight line; each metro line at its own average speed (20 to 28.5 km/h); RER at 60 km/h plus one minute per stop. Waiting is half the interval between trains. In the suburbs, up to 1 km on foot to a station, further at 20 km/h.
  • Result: the quickest time from each point to the hall of each of the four terminals, and to the Nord-Est station taken as the nearer of its two halls.
  • Two stops: for each direction the passenger boards at the home station of the train or at its other Paris stop. Two measures are kept. The plain one is the time to the entrance of the nearest station where the train stops; it is the one shown first on the map. The stricter one adds the minutes the train then takes between its two stops ({{ m.TH.L | 0 }} minutes from Nord-Est to Gare de Lyon and {{ m.TH.M | 0 }} to Montparnasse, stop included assumption); it is the one used to count benefits.
  • Never longer than today: each train still stops where it does now, so no point can lose on either measure. The export script stops with an error if one does.
  • Time inside the station is not in these figures. It is compared separately (see inside the station) and tested as two extra minutes for everyone (see the tests).
  • Weights: one run on the clock; one with the official weights on walking, waiting and changing; one with metro and RER riding counted 25% heavier for crowding.

New passengers are valued at half the time saved, as in every transport appraisal: they gain less than those who already travelled. Yearly figures mix sources that turn a weekday count into a year with factors from 273 to 435, and base years from 2012 to 2025; they are orders of magnitude, not counts.

Official rules that are not applied. A full French appraisal would let the value of time rise with income, discount at 3.2% to 4.6% instead of 3%,[[fsrate]] and count each euro of public money as 1.2 euros.[[dgitm]] The first helps the project and the other two count against it. The central case applies none of them; growth of benefits and the two discount rates are tested in the tests, and growth is used for the comparison with other projects in the economic return.

Cost

Quantities times unit prices from built projects, plus 20% for design and client costs, 7% for land and 30% for risk; the prices, their sources and the test against seven projects are in the cost chapter. Stations are sized by one rule: {{ m.per_track | 0 }} stops an hour per platform track, a reversing train counted as {{ m.service.rev_equiv | 2 }} stops, for the southern lines at their planned capacity (see platforms).

The two accounts, the return and taxes

  • Yearly repayment. Construction cost, increased by interest during {{ m.params.build_years | 0 }} years of even spending, repaid in equal yearly amounts over 60 years at 3% above inflation: each €1 billion costs €{{ m.AF * m.idc * 1000 | 0 }} million a year.
  • Operations account. Costs: running the stations at the rate recorded on the first RER E section (€13,200 a year per metre of platform track) and the tunnels at €{{ m.params.om_tunnel * 1000 | 2 }} million per km, midway between that record (€3.5 million) and the average of the RER network (€1.0 million); one renewal of the tunnels' rail systems after 30 years, set aside evenly; extra running of regional trains. What today's terminals cost to run is not deducted. Income: the charge, track charges on the net change in high-speed journeys, new Navigo passes, regional express fares, the authority's fee, shop rents.
  • The charge. Journeys fall by the fare response: a charge of c on a fare of €{{ b.fare | 2 }} keeps (1 + c / fare) to the power {{ b.fare_response | 1 }} of the journeys.[[cgedd_fares]] The fare is the average revenue per journey before VAT, and the same fare is used for passengers who cross Paris; both overstate the loss. A response of {{ fp.fare_response_low | 1 }} is shown beside the central one. Track charges are taken to follow the net change in journeys assumption. No VAT is counted on the charge.
  • Navigo. New suburban journeys, times the share made by new pass holders, divided by {{ b.pass_trips | 0 }} journeys a year per commuter, times the price of a yearly pass.
  • Capital account. The yearly repayment plus any shortfall of operations, less land sales, a tax on businesses of the size of the Grand Paris Express one and a European grant; what is left is expressed as a grant paid during the works.
  • Economic return. Yearly benefits divided by yearly cost. Benefits are the time saved, comfort and wider effects, less the value of the journeys given up because of the charge. The ticket money of train operators is not counted. With benefits growing g a year, the level yearly amount of the same value over 60 years is used; passenger numbers, and so the charge and the shops, grow at {{ b.traffic_growth_share | pct }} of g, and running costs stay flat assumption. The rate of return is the discount rate at which 60 years of benefits less running costs equal the construction cost.
  • What public budgets get back. VAT at 10% inside the net change in ticket money, less fuel duty on car trips moved to rail. Then three readings of the added activity: none of it is new for the country; or it is counted as in the appraisal the ratios come from (each job worth €10,000 a year of benefits saved, output from denser job markets and from working time saved on the clock taxed at 43.6%); or every job implied is new and taxed on the average output of a job in the region.

The scripts are in the model folder of the repository: nordest.py (access, traffic, cost), finance.py (accounts, return, taxes), export.py (data for this site). src/js/model.js repeats the accounts in the browser and is checked against the Python results to fifteen digits.

What has been checked, and what has not

  • Checked once, on 12 October 2026, and corrected: the two accounts, the passenger charge, the Navigo estimate, what public budgets get back and the comparison with other projects. The reviewer's main corrections were to the running cost of tunnels, now set between two French records where the first version used the higher one; to the tax estimate, now given as three readings; and to the comparison with other projects, whose bases differ. The corrected version has not been checked again.
  • Not checked against outside sources by anyone but their authors: the three annexes on Zürich, the Elizabeth line and the Swiss timetable, and the drawings of stations and works, which are sketches.
  • Access times are times to the entrance of a station. No minutes are added for the depth of platforms; time inside the station is treated separately.
