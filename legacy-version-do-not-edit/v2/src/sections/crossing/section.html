A passenger who crosses Paris today spends about an hour between two trains, with a metro ride in the middle. With the stations joined, a direct train takes {{ m.TH.L | 0 }} to {{ m.connecting.central.pairs['Montparnasse-Lyon'].direct | 0 }} minutes from one side of the city to the other, and a change of train happens on the platforms of one station. TGV passengers who cross Paris

About {{ b.traffic.central.paris_base + b.traffic.central.cross_base | 0 }} million journeys a year by TGV, the French high-speed train, touch Paris: {{ b.traffic.central.paris_base | 0 }} million start or end there and {{ b.traffic.central.cross_base | 1 }} million cross it, changing between two Paris stations, usually by metro or by RER, the express suburban lines that cross Paris underground. Those who cross are counted at two stations, which is why the four terminals count {{ b.traffic.central.paris_base + 2 * b.traffic.central.cross_base | 1 }} million passengers. The {{ b.traffic.central.cross_base | 1 }} million is derived from the last survey, in 2012-13: 13,000 a day in the region, 88% of them in the stations inside Paris, all kinds of train together, scaled by the growth of high-speed traffic since.[[survey]] No newer count is published, and none by pair of stations. Regional and suburban passengers

Suburban lines H, K, P, N and R and the regional express trains end at the four terminals today, unlike the RER. Run through, each train stops at a second station, and passengers stay on board when that station is nearer to where they are going.

Lines H and K from the north run on to Gare de Lyon and join line R. Line P from the east runs on to Montparnasse and joins line N. Regional express trains do the same: those from the north and east continue to Gare de Lyon or Montparnasse, those from the south to Nord-Est.

Trains are assumed to take {{ m.TR.L | 0 }} to {{ m.TR.M | 0 }} minutes between two stations assumption. The saving for each passenger is the difference between the trip from the old terminal to their destination in Paris and the trip from whichever of the two stops is nearer, measured on the public transport network as in reaching the station.

Why two minutes matter

  • Fewer people change onto the metro and the RER at the terminals. Gare du Nord and Gare de l'Est together see 800,000 travellers a day.[[nordest]] A commuter from the northern suburbs who works near Gare de Lyon no longer goes down to RER D at Gare du Nord: the train goes there.
  • Crowding counts. In a survey of Île-de-France passengers, a minute in a full metro weighed up to 1.39 times a minute in an uncrowded one when seated, and 1.55 when standing.[[kroes]] The estimate carries the comfort gained at €{{ m.yearly.comfort | m }} million a year, in the proportion found in the appraisal of the Grand Paris Express.[[sgpi]] The relief of each metro line is not modelled.
  • Not counted: trips from one suburb to another across Paris, which become direct. They need the region's travel model. The traffic these lines could gain is in how many more passengers.
