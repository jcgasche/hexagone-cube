A through station is a station where trains arrive at one end and leave by the other, without turning back. A corridor is a route followed by two or three tunnels side by side, each with two tracks. A portal is the place where a tunnel comes up to join an existing line. An underground junction is a cavern where two tunnels become one. The TGV is the French high-speed train. The RER is the set of express suburban lines that cross Paris underground.

The corridors run from each station to a point beyond the ring road, so that no surface track is needed inside Paris. Choose a layout, switch layers on and off, and select a station or a corridor to read its facts.

Two layouts: three pairs of tracks, or two

The same stations and corridors can be built in two ways. The report prices both.

Layout A: three separate networks

Each corridor has three pairs of tracks: one for suburban trains, one for regional express trains, one for high-speed trains. Nothing is shared. That is {{ m.cost.A.tunnel_km | 1 }} km of two-track tunnel.

Layout B: two networks

Each corridor has two pairs: one for suburban trains, one for all main-line trains (high-speed and regional express together), as on the surface today. That is {{ m.cost.B.tunnel_km | 1 }} km of tunnel. Main-line trains share platforms, which is assumed to save two tracks at each station assumption.

For passengers the two layouts are taken as the same. Layout A keeps a late regional train from delaying a high-speed train. Layout B is how Zürich, Vienna and Stuttgart work,[[zvv, vienna, bt19]] and it costs {{ 1 - m.cost.B.total / m.cost.A.total | pct }} less. Left as it is

  • The five RER lines, A to E, which already cross Paris underground.
  • The metro.
  • Saint-Lazare, Austerlitz and Bercy stations.
  • The train depots, which stay beside the approach lines; see changes at today's stations.

Not included in the cost

  • New trains.
  • Buildings, streets or parks on the land that the surface tracks leave free.
  • Clearing the surface tracks once they are closed.

How the stations sit under the present ones is in the three stations. How all this could be built while trains keep running is in works and stages.
